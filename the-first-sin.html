Mythic Origin

The First Sin

Before the Seven Sins were feared as gods, before relics were hunted like holy wounds carved into the world, there was a single mortal named Pride who decided that being human was a limitation rather than a law.

When Pride Was Still a Man

The oldest surviving records begin in a time when the world was quieter, or at least naive enough to believe it was. In that age, Pride was not yet remembered as a symbol, a caution, or the first shadow cast over the divine. He was simply a man, brilliant enough to be admired and dangerous enough to be remembered. He looked at mortality and saw insult. He looked at the limits placed on flesh, on time, on power, and decided that none of them were final.

That decision is where history tears open. The world of Sinners does not begin with a god creating order from above. It begins with a human hand reaching far past where it was meant to stop.

The Making of the Relics

Pride forged the relics. Not as decorations. Not as simple tools. He made vessels that could hold more than ordinary magic should ever be able to bear. Every telling agrees that the work was obsessive. The relics were crafted to endure, to gather meaning, and to outlive their maker. They were built like promises made against death itself.

At first, they were only objects. That detail matters. The relics were not born awake. They had no voices yet, no cults, no cities bent around them, no clergy bleeding themselves in their names. They were silent at the start. But silence in Sinners is never emptiness. It is waiting.

How Belief Turned Them Into Gods

The relics passed from hand to hand, age to age, story to story. They accumulated fear. Reverence. Rumor. Need. People spoke of them, protected them, hunted them, and wove them into the structure of memory itself. Over time that pressure became enough to wake what had been planted within them. The relics did not merely remain important. They became alive.

That was the birth of the Sins. Not all at once, and not in the order most newcomers guess. Among the relic-born Sins, the remembered awakening order is Sloth first, then Lust, then Wrath, then Envy, then Gluttony, and finally Greed. Pride stands apart from that line because Pride was never relic-born. He came first, but he came first as a mortal.

The Wound That Never Closed

From that moment on, history could no longer behave like ordinary history. Cities would rise under the influence of divine powers that had once been objects. Religions would reshape themselves around beings who were fed by attention. Relics would become the kind of thing entire lives were ruined for. The world did not simply gain new gods. It inherited a wound and learned to build around it.

That is why this record is preserved. The First Sin is not important because it is old. It is important because everything else still bleeds from it. If you want to know why the world is full of marks, miracles, cults, and catastrophes, you begin here, with the first human arrogance great enough to scar reality.

Why the Archive Keeps It

Most stories survive because they are beautiful. This one survives because it is true in the most terrible way possible. It explains too much. It connects too many disasters. It makes the world understandable, and in doing so makes it far less comforting. Pride wanted to outgrow the shape of a human life. Instead, he changed the shape of everyone else's.
