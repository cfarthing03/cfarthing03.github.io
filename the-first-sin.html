Mythic Origin

The First Sin

The oldest story in the archive begins not with a god descending from the heavens, but with a mortal Pride who reached for far more than any person was meant to hold.

Before the Sins

Long before the world spoke of the Sins as divine powers, there was a man remembered as Pride. He was not born a god, nor counted among the relic-born beings that would later shape the age. He was mortal first, which is exactly what makes the story so dangerous. Pride looked at the world and decided that mortality, limits, and endings were insults that could be corrected if one had the will to do so.

The surviving versions of the tale disagree on what Pride originally wanted most. Some say he wanted perfection. Others say he wanted control, immortality, or proof that humanity could surpass every law placed upon it. What all versions agree on is this: he began to work with relics, and in doing so, he created the foundation for everything that came after.

The Forging of the Relics

Pride forged relics as vessels, shaping objects that could hold more than simple magic. They were made to endure, to gather significance, and to outlast the life of the one who created them. In later retellings these relics are spoken of almost as seeds, each carrying the shape of a future power within it. At the time, however, they were not yet gods. They were objects charged with intent, obsession, and possibility.

That distinction matters. The relics did not wake the moment they were made. They waited. They passed through hands, through stories, through worship, through fear. They became important before they became alive, and in Sinners that is often how the most dangerous things begin.

The Awakening

Over time the relics awoke into the beings now known as the Sins. Pride stands apart from them because Pride had been human first. Among the relic-born Sins, the order of awakening remembered in the archive is Sloth, then Lust, then Wrath, then Envy, then Gluttony, and finally Greed. Each awakening changed the world around it, because each new power gave people something real enough to fear, worship, bargain with, or build a life around.

From that point forward, history stopped being a matter of kings, wars, and cities alone. It became the history of what happens when power is fed by attention and when belief begins to cling to a name hard enough that the name starts answering back.

Why This Record Matters

The First Sin is not remembered simply because it is old. It is remembered because nearly every later disaster can trace a line back to it. Relics matter because of this story. Churches hunt meaning because of this story. Entire cities measure themselves against divine powers that would never have existed in the same way without this story.

If you want to understand why the world of Sinners is crowded with relics, gods, marks, miracles, and catastrophes, you begin here. One mortal reached beyond his place, and the world never fully returned to what it had been before.
