The Seven Virtues

Humility

Service, perspective, self-knowledge, and the refusal to confuse importance with superiority.

Humility keeps the silhouette of a weathered white-clad gunslinger, elevated into godhood without losing the restraint that defines him.

To Stand Lower

Humility teaches that worth does not require superiority. To admit ignorance, accept help, serve another person, or step aside so someone else can be heard are all expressions of strength rather than humiliation.

The Gunslinger Saint

Unlike the overwhelming ornament of some Virtues, Humility's god form keeps the identity of a traveler: hat, coat, worn gear, a firearm, and a face marked by experience. Divinity adds scale and symbolism without erasing the person beneath it.

Service Without Applause

His followers often praise work that receives no audience. Cleaning, carrying, listening, burying the dead, repairing what someone else broke, and accepting correction all fit naturally into the faith.

When Humility Becomes Erasure

The danger comes when humility is confused with self-negation. A person can be taught to make themselves smaller forever, to never defend their needs, and to interpret abuse as proof that they still possess too much pride. A virtue about perspective can become a religion of disappearance.
