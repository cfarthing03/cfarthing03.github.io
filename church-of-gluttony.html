Religion of Gluttony

The Church of Gluttony

Food as craft, memory, hospitality, abundance, and one of the simplest ways to care for another person.

Food as Faith

The Church of Gluttony treats cooking as craft, worship, hospitality, and cultural memory. Ingredients are taken seriously, recipes are preserved, and a well-prepared meal can carry as much religious meaning as a formal prayer.

Feeding another person is one of the church's most basic acts of devotion. Food keeps people alive, but a shared meal can also create community, celebrate an event, preserve a memory, or make someone feel welcome.

Hus and Culinary Tradition

The church helped shape Hus into one of the continent's great culinary centers. Public feasts, festivals, charity kitchens, and community meals all reflect the idea that abundance is most meaningful when it can be shared.

That warmth exists alongside Hus's less comfortable reality as the crime capital of the continent. The city contains enormous generosity and enormous appetite, often on the same street.

A Church Rebuilding

The former head of the church was killed by Pride, and the loss caused major upheaval. Many followers left, leaving behind a much smaller congregation attempting to rebuild what had once been a prominent institution.

The current Church Head is a humble gnome, assisted by a resourceful goblin who manages the finances. Their rebuilding has been slow, practical, and far less glamorous than the church's older reputation.

Abundance Without Waste

The church does not teach that endless consumption is inherently holy. The point is fulfillment. Food that is wasted, hoarded while others starve, or consumed without satisfaction misses much of what modern Gluttony represents.

To eat well is good. To feed someone else can be better.
