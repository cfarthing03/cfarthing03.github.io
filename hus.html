The Continent of Sin

Hus

The One Stop Dinner

The continent's largest population center: culinary paradise above, crime capital below.

The One Stop Dinner

Hus is the most densely populated city on the Continent of Sin, and food is the center of nearly everything. Meals are not treated as background necessities. They are entertainment, status, family tradition, business, religion, and civic identity.

The Church of Gluttony

Unlike many cities where a religious order remains partly separate from government, the Church of Gluttony has historically held enormous influence over Hus's food supply. Ingredient quality, distribution, preparation, and culinary standards are all considered matters worthy of religious oversight.

A Standard of Excess

The city's best kitchens are famous throughout the continent. Ingredients arrive from every region, and chefs compete to create food capable of drawing visitors across oceans. Gluttony's influence does not simply encourage eating more. It encourages experiencing more—better flavor, stranger ingredients, larger celebrations, and relentless culinary experimentation.

When the Supply Failed

Hus has already learned what happens when its food systems collapse. A crisis in Church leadership once caused disciples to abandon their posts, leaving an inexperienced successor struggling to keep food moving through the city. Shortages and prices escalated until the Pantheon and surviving Church leadership intervened.

Crime Beneath the Markets

Hus's population density and immense flow of goods make it perfect territory for organized crime. The city is widely regarded as the crime and drug capital of the world, and Life's Blood found a particularly strong market here. The same alleys that lead to extraordinary restaurants can lead just as easily to people selling things that should never have reached the street.

Modern Hus

Hus is rebuilding religious stability, but it has never stopped being Hus. The city remains crowded, loud, delicious, dangerous, and relentlessly hungry. There is almost nothing a person cannot find here if they know whom to ask—and almost nothing that does not come with a price.
