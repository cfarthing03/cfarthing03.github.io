Religion of Envy

The Green Circus

A traveling religion where the tent is the sanctuary, the stage is the altar, and performance is worship.

The Traveling Church

The Green Circus is the primary religion associated with Envy, though calling it a traditional church is generous. It has no permanent temple in the usual sense. The circus itself is the sanctuary, the stage is the altar, and performance is the central act of worship.

Its members include acrobats, clowns, magicians, musicians, actors, dancers, animal trainers, and nearly anyone capable of turning skill into spectacle.

I Wish I Could Do That

The Green Circus embraces one of Envy's central ideas: seeing someone else possess a talent you lack does not have to end in resentment. The thought I wish I could do that can be the beginning of practice, discipline, and improvement.

Performers therefore learn from one another constantly. Imitation is accepted as part of learning, but the eventual goal is to develop an identity and style of your own.

Competition and Collaboration

Healthy competition is common inside the Circus. Performers push one another to improve, invent more elaborate acts, and find new ways to surprise an audience. That competition is balanced by the simple reality that a successful show depends on everyone doing their part.

The result is a faith where comparison and cooperation exist side by side rather than cancelling one another out.

Wonder for Everyone

The Green Circus frequently performs for people who could not otherwise afford entertainment, including poor, isolated, and marginalized communities. They believe wonder should not be restricted to people with enough money to purchase it.

A great performance can inspire, connect, distract, heal, or simply give someone one very good night. To the Circus, all of those are worthwhile forms of worship.
