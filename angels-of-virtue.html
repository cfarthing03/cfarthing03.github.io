Faction · Contingency Wielders

The Angels of Virtue

Seven backup wielders prepared to inherit the powers of the Virtues if one of the original seven fell. They were not the Virtues themselves. They were what came next.

The Contingency Behind the Virtues

The Angels of Virtue were created as a second layer beneath the original seven Virtues. If one of the Virtues died, the power and responsibility attached to that Virtue could not simply be allowed to disappear. Each Angel was therefore tied to one of the seven and prepared as a successor, a backup wielder with a separate identity, separate life, separate power, and in several cases a very different understanding of what the cause meant.

This distinction matters. The Angels of Virtue were not simply junior members of the Virtues and they were not lesser copies. Their abilities did not mirror the powers of the person above them. The connection was one of succession. If the bearer above them was lost, the Angel existed so the chain would continue.

That made the organization a contingency plan disguised as a fellowship. Every friendship inside it existed beside an uncomfortable fact: someone else's death could become your promotion.

Seven People, Not Seven Replacements

What makes the Angels difficult to summarize is that there was never a single personality that defined them. They were selected from radically different lives. Some were already leaders. Some were victims. Some were soldiers. Some were scholars. One did not even know he belonged to the organization until much later.

Their powers were equally strange. One could make distance irrelevant. One could redistribute another person's wounds. One could fire through any material. One knew everything ever written down. One could possess machinery. One could force years of lived experience into a single instant. One could call people from other dimensions and make them believe in his command.

As a unit, they were not elegant. They were terrifyingly complete.

Therion

Virtue of Courage · Bearer of Kindness
Power: Able to teleport anywhere at any time, with the potential to effectively be everywhere at once.

Therion is the anomaly in the organization. Unlike the others, he did not know he was an Angel of Virtue. The role existed around him before he understood that it belonged to him.

When his power finally awakened, that ignorance did not turn into loyalty. Therion ultimately used the very gift meant to make him part of the contingency against Patience and against the Overgod. The backup refused the purpose he had been built to serve.

Tess

Virtue of Responsibility · Bearer of Charity
Power: Can defer injury suffered by another person across any number of willing participants, including herself. She cannot pass damage dealt directly to herself onto anyone else.

Tess was already a leader before the title found her. She led the resistance and is remembered in Patience's records as the first person they brought back to life after death. Practical, shrewd, and comfortable making difficult decisions under pressure, she was trusted to take command if Patience himself died.

That trust was not ceremonial. Tess had a habit of taking control when a situation demanded it, sometimes forgetting that someone else technically held the position above her. Patience did not appear particularly bothered by that. If the room needed a leader, Tess was already leading.

Wyrm

Virtue of Justice · Bearer of Humility
Power: Can shoot through any material and magnify his vision without practical limit to align the perfect shot.

Wyrm was a drow warrior and one of the earliest members of the contingency. The surviving notes describe a bond with Patience closer to brotherhood than simple command. They fought together, worked together, and built trust before the larger plan swallowed their lives.

His loyalty was personal. Wyrm believed in the cause, but much of the force behind that belief came from the person asking him to carry it. Once he committed to a task, he was known for seeing it through to the end.

Saelin

Virtue of Wisdom · Bearer of Patience
Power: Knows everything that has ever been written down in any language. She can also turn people into books.

Saelin was a keeper, a person already defined by knowledge before the gift made that definition literal. She knew an impossible amount and, more importantly, knew how to find what she did not yet know.

She believed in the cause without needing to be emotionally persuaded into it. Her own life, her access to knowledge, and the conclusions she drew from both led her to believe the plan was correct. She became one of the group's strongest sources of intelligence, constantly working to make future operations safer and more informed.

The book transformation is not metaphorical.

Yui

Virtue of Empathy · Bearer of Chastity
Power: Can remotely control machinery and possesses a complete intuitive understanding of machines and how to build them.

Yui came from nobility and hated nearly everything that nobility had done to her. She had been kept isolated, starved, beaten, and treated as a future bargaining piece by a mother obsessed with preserving her value to another wealthy family. When Patience tore through that social order and killed those who refused to surrender their hoarded wealth, Yui watched him execute her mother and saw a rescuer rather than a monster.

She joined him willingly. Technology became the language through which she tried to make weak people dangerous enough to resist stronger oppressors. On one mission, she and Patience armed an entire small community with firearms so they could defend themselves from gangs. When Patience questioned what would happen if the weapons spread, Yui's answer was brutally simple: if good people were truly the majority, they would eventually outnumber the bad.

Her humor was strange, her sense of boundaries underdeveloped, and Patience found both qualities genuinely funny. Their relationship read less like commander and subordinate and more like an older brother trying to keep up with an extremely dangerous younger sister.

Mourn

Virtue of Gravity · Bearer of Diligence
Power: Can force someone to experience years of life in an instant and choose how long the resulting stun lasts.

Mourn had once served as the right hand of a king whose mind deteriorated into paranoia. The king forced him to produce supposed conspirators for execution under threat that Mourn's own family would be killed if he refused. He complied for months. Then his wife was discovered as an actual conspirator and the threat became real. His family, including his children, was executed.

Patience eventually arrived and killed the king. Mourn saw the act as angelic justice and attached himself to the cause with an intensity that bordered on worship.

Despite everything he had survived, Mourn refused to kill casually. His gift made restraint possible. During one negotiation in Rushinghan, he crossed a room in less than a second, touched every hostile man between himself and the mob boss, and left the entire group standing motionless while their minds endured years elsewhere. When asked how long they would remain that way, Mourn answered: two years.

Seric

Virtue of Humanity · Bearer of Temperance
Power: Can pull people from other dimensions into the world, from heroes and soldiers to commoners, with those summoned fully believing in Humanity's orders and fighting for his cause.

Seric had been a military officer defending one of Palin's larger villages from raiders and slavers. He was disciplined, religious, and exceptionally good at organizing defense. For years the village held. Then the raider groups united, overwhelmed the defenses, and put the survivors in cages.

Patience arrived in angelic form, hunted down the slavers, and freed the captives. Seric immediately demanded to be allowed to help do the same work elsewhere.

He remained religious even after joining the Overgod's plan. He prayed to Sloth for the dead and prayed to the Overgod for the people he himself had to kill or summon into battle. He could accept violence as necessary while still treating every death as something that required acknowledgement. This made him both a powerful commander and an exhausting traveling companion. After one battle, Patience had to sit through separate prayers for the same corpses because Seric refused to risk sending anyone into the next life improperly.

Not the Virtues

The name causes confusion because it is supposed to. The Angels of Virtue existed beside the Virtues, not instead of them. Patience, Kindness, Charity, Diligence, Chastity, Temperance, and Humility were the primary bearers. Therion, Tess, Wyrm, Saelin, Yui, Mourn, and Seric were the prepared second line.

That second line was never simple insurance. They had opinions. They formed attachments. They disagreed. They changed. Therion's eventual rebellion proved the central flaw in treating people as contingencies: a backup is still a person, and a person can decide that the role prepared for them is wrong.
