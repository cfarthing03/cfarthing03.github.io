The Seven Sins

Gluttony

Nourishment, fulfillment, abundance—and the difference between being full and being satisfied.

Gluttony is one of the most mysterious Sins. His enormous, boar-like body is famous, but the true extent of what he can consume makes ordinary hunger seem almost irrelevant.

The Hunger That Can Eat Anything

Gluttony can consume almost anything. Food and physical matter are the obvious examples, but his appetite can extend to living creatures, energy, knowledge, and even things that are difficult to describe as physical objects at all. When he consumes something, he can gain qualities, abilities, or attributes from it.

That makes his power frighteningly open-ended. It also makes him difficult to understand. Gluttony's hunger is not merely a need for calories. It is a mechanism through which he experiences, acquires, and becomes.

What Gluttony Represents

Modern Gluttony is associated with nourishment, fulfillment, abundance, and experiencing life fully. Hunger is treated as a signal that something is missing. The important question is what you use to fill that absence.

A person can eat until they cannot move and still feel empty. Someone else can share a modest meal with people they love and leave completely satisfied. Gluttony's faith lives in the difference.

Hus

Hus is the largest city on the continent by population. Its public identity is warm, communal, and heavily centered on food. Feasts, restaurants, public meals, hospitality, and culinary tradition make the city one of the easiest places to associate with Gluttony's more generous aspects.

It is also the crime capital of the continent. That is less contradiction than mathematics. Hus has the most people, so it also has the most thieves, gangs, smugglers, criminal networks, and opportunities for someone to decide they are hungry for something that belongs to somebody else.

The Church of Gluttony

Gluttony's organized church has historically treated cooking as craft, worship, memory, and hospitality. Ingredients are scrutinized, recipes preserved, public meals organized, and feeding another person can be treated as a genuine religious act.

The church lost much of its prominence after its former leader was killed by Pride. What remains is smaller and still rebuilding, currently led by a humble gnome with the help of a resourceful goblin who manages the finances. For a faith built around abundance, rebuilding from scarcity has become its own lesson.
