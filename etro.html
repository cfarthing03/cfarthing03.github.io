Etro

City of Dreams

Entertainment, illusion, music, and a criminal history hiding beneath a spotless surface.

The City of Dreams

Etro sits apart from the rest of the Continent of Sin, but its isolation has never kept it from influencing the wider world. The city is built around entertainment, performance, spectacle, and the promise that anyone can step into a life more exciting than the one they arrived with. It was once closely associated with Envy, and that history still lingers in the way Etro encourages people to look at what others have, imagine something greater, and then become it.

The Radio Tower

The Radio Tower dominates both the skyline and the city's daily rhythm. Twelve songs are broadcast from the tower again and again, filling streets, businesses, homes, and public spaces. Citizens are expected to carry city-issued pins that keep them connected to the broadcast. Over time the music becomes so ordinary that many residents barely notice it, even as visitors immediately feel as though they have stepped into a musical already in progress.

A City That Performs

Etro's streets are exceptionally clean, its public spaces carefully maintained, and spontaneous entertainment is common. Dancing, theater, live music, public acts, and elaborate venues make the city one of the continent's most popular destinations. Residents often seem impossibly cheerful to outsiders. Whether that happiness is entirely natural, socially expected, or simply practiced well enough to become real depends on who you ask.

The Life's Blood Trade

Etro's brightness has always had a shadow. The city was once deeply connected to the trade of Life's Blood, an extraordinarily dangerous substance that attracted criminals, performers, and people desperate for an advantage. Fame and fortune were sometimes tied to brutal initiations and organizations that blurred entertainment, exploitation, and crime.

Behind the Curtain

Modern Etro remains a beautiful place, but its reputation is built on contradiction. It is genuinely joyful, genuinely artistic, and genuinely capable of making visitors feel like anything is possible. It is also a city that became very good at making ugly things look beautiful. Knowing which version of Etro you are seeing is part of surviving it.
