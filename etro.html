The Continent of Sin

Etro

City of Dreams

Entertainment, illusion, music, and a criminal history hiding beneath a spotless surface.

At the Table: Arriving in Etro

The first thing your players hear is music. It reaches them before the city fully comes into view, faint at first, then impossible to ignore. By the time they reach Etro proper, the music seems to come from everywhere at once. Shop interiors, public squares, street corners, theaters, restaurants, private balconies, and the specialized pins worn throughout the city all carry pieces of the same larger broadcast.

The streets look almost staged. Pavement is clean. Signs are polished. Windows are carefully arranged. Performers occupy public spaces as if every intersection might become a stage. A dancer rehearses beside a fountain. A singer is arguing with a manager outside a venue. Someone has drawn a crowd around a card trick. Someone else is crying beside a poster with their own face on it.

Etro feels like a city permanently waiting for the audience to arrive, except the audience is already everywhere.

The City of Dreams

Etro is geographically separated from much of the continent, and that isolation helped it develop into one of the most culturally distinct cities in the world. Entertainment is not an industry that happens to be successful here. It is the city's identity.

Music, performance, theater, celebrity, public competition, spectacle, and image shape everything from architecture to social status. People come to Etro because they want to be seen, because they want to see someone else, or because they want to become the kind of person other people watch.

This is where Envy's influence is easiest to understand. Etro is built around looking at what another person can do and deciding what that awakens in you.

The Radio Tower

The Radio Tower stands at the center of Etro's cultural life. Twelve songs are broadcast continuously, repeated throughout the city through the pins residents and visitors are expected to wear. To someone who has lived in Etro for years, the music can become almost invisible. To a newcomer, it is immediate and strange.

The songs become part of the city's shared memory. People associate particular streets, relationships, performances, and moments of their lives with pieces of the broadcast. A song can become old and familiar without ever truly disappearing.

The Tower also means Etro has a shared rhythm. Everyone is hearing the same thing. Everyone is connected to the same cultural signal. The city is constantly listening together.

A City Built to Be Seen

Etro's architecture reflects its obsession with presentation. Public squares are designed to gather crowds. Major streets open toward performance venues. Buildings use balconies, terraces, glass, banners, lighting, and decorative facades to make themselves memorable.

Even ordinary businesses understand spectacle. A restaurant might hire musicians. A clothing shop may turn new inventory into a public fashion show. A tavern may stage nightly competitions. A bakery can become famous because the owner figured out how to turn breakfast into theater.

Reputation moves fast here. A talented performer can become known across the city in days. A public failure can spread just as quickly.

Competition and Comparison

Etro encourages people to compare themselves constantly. Who is more talented? Who is more beautiful? Who is more popular? Who has the larger audience? Who got the role? Who sold out the venue? Who is being talked about tonight?

That pressure can be cruel, but it can also be productive. Envy's modern interpretation is not simply resentment. It is the spark that comes from seeing another person accomplish something and thinking, I want to be able to do that too.

Etro is full of people trying to become better because someone else proved better was possible.

The Underworld Beneath the Spotlight

Etro's clean streets and bright venues hide an old criminal network. Entertainment creates money, crowds, private access, desperate ambition, and people willing to take risks to become successful. Criminal organizations learned to use all of those things.

The historic Life's Blood trade flourished in parts of Etro because performers, competitors, criminals, and desperate people all had reasons to seek an advantage. Backstage rooms, private clubs, exclusive parties, and supposedly legitimate entertainment businesses sometimes overlapped with much darker operations.

The city is excellent at controlling presentation. That is exactly why its ugliest elements can hide so effectively.

Living in Etro

For residents, Etro can be exhilarating. There is always something happening. There is always someone new to watch, something new to hear, or some new rivalry taking shape. A person can leave home without plans and find an entire evening simply by following the nearest crowd.

It can also be exhausting. In a city built around attention, being ignored can feel like failure. People reinvent themselves constantly. Careers rise and collapse quickly. The pressure to remain interesting can become its own kind of hunger.

Etro sells dreams very well. The city is less interested in warning people what those dreams might cost.
