Tiefling

Tieflings are touched by infernal blood, but in Sinners that does not make them pariahs by default. Their presence is often taken as lucky, their charm as reassuring, and their arrival as a sign that fortune may be shifting in a welcome direction.

Infernal lineageGood omenFortune-touched

Public Perception

In many worlds, a tiefling walks into town and is greeted with fear. In Sinners, the reaction can be the opposite. Horns, tails, and infernal features are commonly read as signs of resilience, luck, and strange favor. Some people still distrust anything powerful, but the average superstition leans in the tiefling’s favor rather than against it.

Why They Are Welcomed

The moral assumptions of this world do not line up neatly with old heavenly stories. Because of that, the infernal is not automatically treated as evil. Tieflings are often seen as travelers who carry opportunity with them. Merchants may quietly hope a tiefling enters first on market day. Sailors may ask one to bless a departing voyage. Gamblers may refuse to start a game until one has taken a seat nearby.

Common Traits

Tieflings vary widely in appearance, but horns, tails, unusual eyes, and a natural sense of dramatic presence are all common. Even when dressed simply, many tieflings stand out. Some lean into that reputation with style and performance. Others wear it lightly and let the superstition work for them.

Place in the World

Because they are more often welcomed than feared, tieflings can be found in nearly every corner of the world. You may meet them as priests, gamblers, merchants, guides, wanderers, or performers. Whatever role they take, stories tend to gather around them faster than around most people.
