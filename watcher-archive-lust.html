The Second Voice

Lust remembers waking as a pink lovebird and immediately knowing two impossible facts: she was alive, and the exhausted purple creature across the room was like her.

She did not know his history. She did not know what Pride had done. She simply knew he was not an object.

Lust // Watcher interview 02-1

He looked at me like I had just walked in carrying the sun.

I thought I had done something impressive.

I hadn't. I was just there.

He'd been alone so long that “there” was enough.

Her earliest personality is visible even through Pride's hostile notes. She asked questions constantly. She named instruments Pride had only numbered. She decorated the edge of a containment slate with scratches that Pride later classified as “nonfunctional markings.” She tried to make Sloth laugh and succeeded often enough for Pride to separate them when he wanted either one easier to control.

REC-P/L02-07 // behavioral noteauthorship: Pride

L-02 social fixation on S-01 is reducing task efficiency.

L-02 repeatedly interrupts procedures to address S-01 directly.

S-01 displays improved activity while L-02 is present and pronounced withdrawal when L-02 is removed.

Recommendation: preserve association. It may be useful.

Watcher annotation: Pride identified friendship as leverage before either Sin understood he had done so.

What She Saw

Lust's account of Sloth's correction is catalogued here rather than in Sloth's file because it is her memory, not his. Sloth remembers pain in fragments. Lust remembers the room.

Lust // Watcher interview 02-4

You want details because details prove it happened.

Fine.

The table had grooves cut into it. I thought they were decoration the first time. They were channels. So the blood wouldn't pool under him.

Subject requested water. Interview resumed after 6 minutes.

Pride opened him from the sternum down. Not fast. He'd learned by then that fast made the edges pull back together. So he used clamps to keep them apart.

Sloth could see inside himself. That's the part I need you to write. He could see what Pride was doing because Pride angled the mirror.

According to Lust, Pride would speak throughout the procedure in the same tone he used when discussing weather, measurements, or failed equipment. He announced what he was cutting because he wanted to know whether Sloth could still identify the sensation after enough of his body had been separated.

Lust // same interview

There was a sound when he pulled the muscle away from the ribs.

Not a scream. The other sound.

Wet. Sticky.

Sloth screamed after.

I used to think screaming was one noise. It isn't.

There is the noise when pain surprises you. There is the noise when you know it's coming. There is the noise when you are begging without using words.

By the end he had one that barely sounded like him.

Lust describes seeing Sloth's severed hand on a metal tray, fingers flexing in time with the arm that was no longer attached to it. Pride inserted a probe beneath a fingernail and watched Sloth's face to confirm sympathetic sensation. When Sloth turned his head away, Pride repositioned the tray.

She describes sections of tissue suspended in glass vessels while Sloth's remaining body strained toward them. She describes a jaw removed and restored incorrectly so that when he tried to speak, his teeth met at the wrong angle. She describes Pride correcting the alignment only after he had finished recording how long Sloth would keep trying to answer.

Lust

Once his throat was gone and he was still trying to scream.

No sound. Just his chest doing it.

Pride leaned down and said, “See? Better.”

Interview suspended by Watcher lead. Subject resumed voluntarily the following day.
INT-L02-05Behavioral notes

The Part That Made Her Afraid

The gore was not what frightened Lust most.

What frightened her was how ordinary Pride could become immediately afterward.

Lust // interview 02-5

He'd wash his hands.

That's what I remember.

Sloth would be on the table trying to hold his own face together and Pride would wash his hands.

Then sometimes he'd fix everything. Clean him. Wrap him up. Talk to him. Call him cooperative for staying still even though staying still was the only thing he could do.

Sloth would look relieved.

And I would hate him for looking relieved.

Then I'd hate myself because of course he was relieved.

Pride's kindness taught Lust exactly what punishment was for. It was not loss of control. It was control performed with enough precision to leave the victim capable of hoping the controller might stop.

When Pride eventually gave Lust a project of her own, she already knew what refusal could cost.

The Order

The Rixel Virus was conceived because Sloth had proven an unacceptable fact: a useful person could choose not to obey.

Pride's directive to Lust is one of the most complete surviving documents in the archive. It does not ask for a disease in the ordinary sense. It asks for an obedience system that can spread.

DIR-RIX-01 // design requirementsclassification: coercive control

1. Identify or exploit the host's strongest desire.

2. Use that desire to bias decision-making before overt control is necessary.

3. If persuasion fails, assume direct control of motor function.

4. Preserve host viability as long as practical. A dead host cannot act, travel, or spread.

5. Create external structures capable of transmitting the condition by penetration or contact.

6. Resistance must not free the host.

Final handwritten note: “The S-01 failure cannot be permitted to repeat in a useful asset.”

Lust knew exactly what those requirements meant.

She did not know where Pride intended to release the finished system or who he planned to control with it. She did know that she was being asked to make something capable of turning a conscious body into equipment.

Lust // interview 03-2

I need this stated correctly.

I did not accidentally make it dangerous.

I knew what it did.

I didn't know what he was going to do with it.

Those are different sentences.

Both matter.

Building the Rixel Virus

Early versions focused on desire. The organism—or spell, or living magical structure; Watcher researchers still dispute the best category—learned what the host wanted most and pushed thoughts toward it. The manipulation was subtle enough that a host could believe the choice remained entirely their own.

Pride considered subtlety useful but insufficient.

The later design grew pink ribbon-like structures through tears, openings, and breaks in the skin. Internally, those ribbons threaded alongside muscle and tendon. Externally, they could pierce or contact another body and establish infection. Once developed, the ribbons did not merely pull from outside. They functioned as an additional set of tendons.

RIX-L02 / host trial 14 // Lust's hand preserved beside Pride's notationLust: Host reports that the right arm is moving “before I decide to move it.” Ribbon tension visible beneath forearm skin.

Pride: Increase command conflict. Determine whether voluntary resistance can overpower external tendon network.

Lust: I do not recommend increasing conflict at this stage.

Pride: Noted. Increase it.

The answer was no.

Resistance made the virus more horrifying. A victim's natural muscles pulled one direction while the ribbon network pulled another. Joints became the argument between them. The host could remain fully conscious while their own body broke around the disagreement.

Lust // interview 03-6

I heard the shoulder go before I understood what I was hearing.

The host was pulling backward. The ribbon pulled forward.

There was a pop and then the arm hung wrong, but it still moved.

That's what made me sick.

Broken didn't mean free.

Preservation protocols were then strengthened. The virus learned to keep a host alive through damage that should have ended the trial. Pride's reason was practical: living bodies moved farther, transmitted more reliably, and remained useful longer.

INT-L02-07Ethics annotation

Why She Kept Working

Lust's testimony refuses the two easy versions of her story.

She does not claim innocence. She also does not accept the claim that coercion is irrelevant because her hands performed the work.

Lust // interview 03-7

I kept seeing Sloth on that table.

Every time I thought about refusing, I could hear him.

Not metaphorically. I could hear him. Pride made sure we could.

So I told myself: finish the work, stay useful, stay off the table, maybe stay close enough to help him later.

Then I told myself Pride wanted a threat, not something he'd actually use.

Then I told myself the next improvement would make the last one less necessary.

I got very good at telling myself things.

Watcher interviewers repeatedly offered her language such as forced, coerced, and under threat. She accepted all three and still insisted the archive retain the sentence: I made it.

Watcher annotationResponsibility and coercion are both factual. Removing either one makes the file easier to read and less accurate.
Modern Rixel samplesContinuity analysis

It Never Became Something Else

The modern Rixel Virus is not a distant descendant that drifted into greater cruelty. Its core behavior matches Lust's original finished design.

  • Transmission still occurs through contact with the ribbons that emerge from infected bodies.
  • The virus still identifies and exploits deep desire before escalating to direct motor control.
  • Ribbon structures still function as additional tendons capable of forcing movement.
  • The host is still maintained alive as long as possible to maximize utility and spread.
  • Resistance can still break the victim's own bones without restoring control.

Lust did not know Pride's eventual deployment plan. She did know she had built a mechanism for control without consent. The distinction is why her later divine identity centers so intensely on chosen bonds, love, friendship, family, and desire that belongs to the person feeling it.

Personhood noteClosing interview

The Person Behind the File

It is easy to read Lust's archive and imagine a frightened technician. The surviving interviews show someone more complicated: talkative when nervous, quick to joke when a room becomes too heavy, furious at people who describe affection as weakness, and still capable of becoming visibly excited when discussing art made for someone rather than for an audience.

She watched a friend be turned into a lesson and then spent years terrified that she would become the next lesson.

She also remained the person who, upon waking, asked Sloth what he wanted to be called.

Lust // final answer, interview 03-9

Pride thought wanting something made you easy to control.

He wasn't completely wrong.

That's why choice matters.

If I love you, I don't get to turn that into ownership.

If you love me, you still get to say no.

I learned that from the worst possible teacher.
