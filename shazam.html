Person of Note · Legendary War Horse

Shazam

A war horse of unprecedented power, remembered less as an animal that followed heroes into battle and more as a force that chose, on occasion, to permit heroes to ride beside him.

The Name Before the Thunder

There are names that announce a person. Shazam's announced weather. When his name was spoken, lightning was said to lance down from the sky and thunder answered in anticipation. Whether this was some magic tied directly to him or simply another impossible quality that followed in his wake is never satisfactorily explained in the surviving record. It was treated, instead, as one more fact of his existence.

Even without the storm, he was difficult to mistake for an ordinary horse. Shazam was built with the kind of presence that made the word "horse" feel technically correct and somehow insufficient. Everything about him suggested that he had been made for more than grazing fields, pulling carts, or carrying soldiers who happened to own the reins.

A Horse Who Understood

Shazam understood human speech. That much appears repeatedly enough in the surviving accounts to be treated as fact. Whether he could answer in kind is another matter. He never did, or perhaps simply never considered it necessary. A neigh was generally the most anyone received in return.

Strangely, that was often enough. Riders and companions learned to understand tone, timing, refusal, approval, and insult without requiring a single spoken word from him. The distinction between "could not speak" and "would not speak" remains unresolved, and after enough stories about him, it becomes difficult to believe the distinction mattered to Shazam at all.

Living Metal

His armor was no ordinary barding. Shazam wore living metal, a rare material capable of reshaping itself according to the will of the wearer, provided the mind directing it possessed enough fortitude to command the change. In his possession, the armor seems to have had almost no practical limitation recorded.

It could become protection, transportation, or a weapon. Accounts describe it forming a sidecar so passengers could travel beside him, then in other circumstances becoming something capable of catastrophic force. The same armor that could turn a war horse into a mobile weapon platform could also be used for something far more humiliating.

The Kiddy Car

One of the most persistent stories surrounding Shazam concerns riders he did not consider worthy but who, for one reason or another, still needed to travel with him. Rather than permit such a person the dignity of riding normally, the living metal could form what later accounts describe as a tiny child's riding car.

The seat moved its rider gently up and down as they traveled. Slowly. Repeatedly. Publicly. The humiliation appears to have been entirely intentional. No surviving account records Shazam explaining the decision.

The War Against Pride

Shazam is remembered as one of the figures who joined the war against Pride to take the world back. That alone would have been enough to preserve his name. Instead, the stories surrounding him survived because he never behaved like someone's mount dragged into history by the importance of whoever sat on his back. He had standards of his own, decisions of his own, and more power than a creature documented as an ordinary horse had any obvious right to possess.

Two Thousand Years Later

The strangest part of the record comes at its end. For all the legends, all the impossible armor, all the thunder that answered his name, Shazam is still documented as a normal horse.

His body has never been recovered.

More than two thousand years have passed, and there is still no confirmed grave, remains, or final account that explains what became of him. Perhaps that means nothing. Perhaps somewhere, after everything else has changed, a door opens onto a road beneath gathering clouds and Shazam is still moving across the world.
