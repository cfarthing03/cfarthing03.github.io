Overgod's Revenge · Historical Catastrophe

The Calamity

During Overgod's Revenge, Temperance sacrificed a portion of the continent to summon the Horseman of Death. The land was not destroyed. It was deleted from reality.

I remember thinking the morning was too clear.

There were no storms. No marching army. No warning bells. Nothing that made the day feel important enough to become the day I would spend the rest of my life remembering.

I was looking toward the coast when it happened.

There was no explosion.

That is what people always get wrong.

They ask how loud it was. They ask whether the ground shook. They ask whether there was fire.

There was nothing.

One moment there was land. Hills. Roads. Trees. Buildings small enough that I could not tell whose they were from where I stood. The next moment all of it was gone.

Not falling. Not burning. Gone.

My eyes kept trying to find the edge of something that no longer existed.

Then the water moved.

For several heartbeats the world was completely quiet, and then the sea realized there was somewhere new for it to be.

I heard the water before I understood what I was seeing. It rushed inward from every side, pouring into the absence where the continent had been. It sounded enormous. Not like a wave. Like the ocean itself had been given permission to fall.

That was the first sound of the Calamity.

People started naming who had been there.

Someone said their brother's village was on that side. Someone else started screaming that the road their daughter had taken ran through it. A man beside me kept repeating the name of a town as though saying it enough times might force the world to remember it.

There were no ruins to search.

No bodies to bury.

No smoke to follow home.

We could not even point at where they died because the place itself had been taken with them.

I still hate quiet rooms.

Years later, sudden silence makes my stomach turn.

People say Temperance sacrificed the land to summon Death. Historians argue over the size of the missing territory, the number of people lost, and what exactly it means to remove a place from reality.

I do not care what word they use.

I watched the world have a piece cut out of it so cleanly that for a few seconds even the screaming had not started yet.

Sometimes I think those seconds were the worst part.

Archive Note: The identity of the writer is unknown. The journal was recovered years after the event, its final pages repeatedly damaged by water.
