Keeper · Survivor · Extremely Questionable Guardian

Kevin the Keeper

The last surviving Keeper to emerge from Hilnem's Great Kaiju Battle, Kevin was never the strongest person traveling with the heroes of Overgod's Revenge. He was simply very difficult to get rid of.

The Last Keeper

Kevin served among the Keepers during the era of Overgod's Revenge. When Hilnem became the site of the Great Kaiju Battle, nearly every Keeper caught in the disaster was killed. Kevin survived.

That survival did not suddenly make him powerful. Compared with many of the people around him, Kevin remained relatively weak. What he did have was persistence, knowledge of the Keepers, and a willingness to follow the party into situations where sensible people generally stopped following.

He eventually traveled with the party and assisted their attempt to reach the Overgod, becoming less a great champion and more the sort of companion who kept finding a way to remain useful when the scale of events had long since outgrown him.

Kevin and Justice

At some point during the journey, Kevin was separated from the rest of the party alongside their sentient car, Justice, better known by the deeply dignified name Truck-Kun.

The pair were stranded in the wastes of Palin. What followed is generally described in surviving accounts as a period of adaptation, survival, and increasingly concerning decision-making.

Kevin and Justice gathered abandoned orphans scattered throughout the wastes and formed them into a roaming warband. The result was something between a refugee caravan, a family, and a Mad Max story that no responsible historian should attempt to defend in court.

The Wasteland Warband

The group survived by moving constantly, scavenging what the desert offered, protecting one another, and developing a relationship with conventional law that can most charitably be described as creative.

Later retellings credit the warband with an impressive number of actions that would violate international humanitarian law in worlds where such conventions actually exist. The children involved generally remember this period more fondly than the adults attempting to catalogue it.

ARCHIVAL NOTE: Repeated references to “war crimes” appear to be both technically alarming and narratively enthusiastic. No attempt has been made to count them.

Legacy

Kevin's story is strange because it never required him to become the strongest person in the room. He survived the destruction of his order, traveled beside heroes fighting forces far beyond ordinary mortals, got lost in a desert with a sentient automobile, and somehow responded by becoming the guardian of a wasteland family.

There are grander names in the histories of Overgod's Revenge. Very few of them can claim Kevin's particular résumé.
