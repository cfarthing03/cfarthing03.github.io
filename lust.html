The Seven Sins

Lust

Desire, connection, affection, passion, and the choice to reach for another person.

Lust is the goddess of desire in its broadest sense. Romance is part of her domain, but so are friendship, art, ambition, intimacy, affection, and every bond strong enough to make someone care.

The Goddess of Connection

Lust represents desire, love, passion, and the connections people choose to form with one another. In Rushinghan, her name does not carry the automatic shame that it once did. Desire can be romantic, sexual, artistic, familial, social, creative, or deeply personal. Wanting something is not itself a moral failure.

The central question in Lust's modern worship is choice. A connection only has meaning if the people involved are allowed to choose it. That belief is especially important because Lust's own history contains one of the clearest examples of what happens when desire is stripped of consent.

The Rixel Virus

Before her godhood, Lust was assigned by Pride to develop what would become the Rixel Virus. Its purpose was to identify a person's deepest desires and use those desires to influence behavior. What began as psychological manipulation eventually became something far more horrifying.

In its developed form, pink ribbons emerged from gaps and breaks in the victim's skin, threading through the body like additional tendons. The ribbons could physically force the host to move. Resistance only increased the pressure, breaking bones while the victim remained conscious inside a body that no longer obeyed them.

When the true danger of the virus became impossible to ignore, Lust abandoned Pride alongside the other Sins. The experience left a permanent mark on her divine identity. The goddess of desire is now defined just as much by the right to say no as by the freedom to say yes.

Ribbons, Reclaimed

Ribbons remain common in Lust's imagery, but their meaning has changed. Where the Rixel Virus used ribbons as restraints, modern depictions often use them as symbols of voluntary bonds: relationships, promises, communities, and the invisible connections between people who choose one another.

That contrast is one of the clearest summaries of Lust herself. Desire can connect people or control them. The difference is choice.

Rushinghan

Rushinghan is one of the continent's great centers of art, music, relationships, and creative expression. Lust's influence encourages people to care loudly and openly. Friendships, romances, passions, and personal expression are treated as things worth celebrating rather than hiding.

The Rebellion also began here, not because its people rejected the Sins, but because they loved them. In Rushinghan, caring about something enough to fight for it makes perfect sense.
