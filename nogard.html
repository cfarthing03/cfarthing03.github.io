Person of Note · Proprietor of Nogard's Treasure Trove

Nogard

Some merchants wait for customers to find them. Nogard's shop does not. Open the wrong door at the right moment and you may find yourself standing inside a store that was never on the other side. Shelves stretch with magical objects, impossible curiosities, and exactly the sort of thing you did not know you needed until the old man behind the counter starts naming his price.

Everything Has a PriceNogard loves a bargain almost as much as he loves winning one. Haggling with him is less a transaction and more a contest he fully expects to enjoy.
The Door Chooses WhenThere is no reliable road to the Treasure Trove. Any ordinary door may become the entrance when the shop wishes to be found.
He Knows What You NeedNogard has an irritating habit of pointing visitors toward the path they actually need, whether or not they understand why yet.

The Merchant

At first glance Nogard is easy to dismiss as a deranged old shopkeeper with an excellent inventory and an even better instinct for separating adventurers from their gold. He bargains relentlessly, pushes prices as far as he can, and treats every negotiation like a private sport. Trying to out-haggle him can become a battle of wits all its own, and Nogard is usually having far more fun than the person on the other side of the counter.

That performance is convincing because so much of it is genuine. Nogard truly does love the sale. He truly does enjoy the argument. Gold matters to him, or at least he is very committed to making everyone believe that it does. Yet the longer someone spends around him, the harder it becomes to believe that profit is the whole reason he keeps opening the door.

Nogard's Treasure Trove

The shop itself is as famous as the man. It is not reached by a street, district, or road. Instead, people find it by accident, by need, or by whatever strange rule governs Nogard's willingness to receive them. A bedroom door, tavern entrance, cellar hatch, or doorway in a place that should lead somewhere completely ordinary may instead open into the Treasure Trove.

Inside, the distinction between rare and impossible becomes difficult to maintain. Magical items fill the shelves in quantities that should make collectors, monarchs, and churches deeply uncomfortable. Nogard seems to possess almost anything, and when he does not immediately produce what a visitor asks for, there is often the unsettling sense that he could.

An Unreliable Fool Who Is Almost Never Wrong

Nogard's behavior makes him easy to underestimate. He rambles, jokes, bargains, and often presents himself like an old man whose greatest concern is squeezing a few more coins from a customer. Then, somewhere in the middle of an otherwise ridiculous conversation, he says exactly the thing someone needed to hear.

Again and again, Nogard has pointed adventurers in the direction they needed to go. Sometimes his advice is direct. Sometimes it only becomes meaningful much later. He rarely explains how he knows what he knows, and trying to force a clear answer out of him is usually less productive than surviving one of his sales pitches.

The Longer You Stay

The strange parts are easiest to notice when a visit lasts too long. Nogard refers casually to details he should not know. He mentions events he was never present for. He talks about possibilities as though he has already watched them happen, including things that have not happened yet and things that may never happen at all.

Visitors who notice this tend to leave with more questions than answers. Nogard, naturally, is happy to sell them something before they go.

When He Actually Intervenes

Nogard prefers guidance to action. He nudges, redirects, equips, warns, and waits. Direct intervention is rare enough to be noteworthy, but it has happened when circumstances became desperate enough. Even then, Nogard has spoken as though helping is not simply a matter of whether he wants to. There are rules. There is an order that must remain where it is. What those rules are, who established them, and what happens if Nogard ignores them are questions he does not answer.

NOGARD

There is no accession number for this file. No curator remembers cataloguing it. The paper version has never been found, yet this record continues to appear whenever the correct mark is noticed. Attempts to copy it produce incomplete text. Attempts to remove it have, so far, failed.

Initial Observation

The public description of Nogard is not false. That may be the problem. He bargains, complains, laughs at his own jokes, and behaves with such consistency that the easiest conclusion is that there is nothing beneath the performance. Several observers reached that conclusion. Their later notes become considerably less certain.

Repeated visits suggest that Nogard knows information he could not reasonably possess. In isolation, any single incident can be dismissed as luck, rumor, or a merchant's instinct. Collected together, they become difficult to explain.

ENTRY 04: He answered a question that had not been asked aloud.
ENTRY 09: He referenced a conversation conducted ██████████████ away.
ENTRY 11: He described an event scheduled for the following week. The event never occurred.
ENTRY 12: He later asked why we had "chosen the other version."

The Location Problem

The Treasure Trove cannot be placed on any reliable map. Doors open into it from locations that share no geography, no architecture, and in several cases no plausible physical connection. Measurements taken inside the shop do not reconcile with the dimensions of any structure used to enter it.

The working hypothesis is that the shop is not hidden somewhere in the world. It is elsewhere.

TEST: Divine communication attempted inside premises.
RESULT: No response.

TEST: Secondary divine authority invoked.
RESULT: No response.

TEST: ██████████████
RESULT: The shopkeeper looked toward the observer and said, "Don't."

Nothing in the available notes proves why divine influence fails inside the Trove. The more disturbing detail is that the failure does not appear limited to ordinary gods. One damaged report extends the same observation to ██████████████. The next three pages are missing.

Classification Dispute

Several authors attempted to classify Nogard by comparing him to known divine hierarchies. None of the labels remained stable. The closest recurring comparison is to an Overgod in scale, but every surviving annotation immediately rejects the title itself.

POWER: ████████
STATUS: NOT GOD
STATUS: NOT OVERGOD
STATUS: ██████████████

NOTE IN DIFFERENT HAND: "Stop trying to name things you don't understand."

Whether the correction came from another researcher is unknown. The handwriting does not match any registered contributor to the archive.

Intervention

Nogard normally avoids direct involvement. He provides objects, information, warnings, and inconveniently precise directions, then allows events to continue without him. There are exceptions. In the few records where he intervened personally, circumstances were already described as desperate.

When questioned afterward, he referred to rules. Not laws. Not promises. Rules. He has never provided a complete list.

"If I fix every little problem, there won't be an order left to put back."

Attribution: Nogard?
Confidence: ███████

No observer agrees on what he meant by "order." One theory assumes a cosmic hierarchy. Another assumes chronology. A third page contains only the words ██████████████ repeated until the ink tears through the paper.

The Yellow Mark

A recurring symbol appears throughout surviving versions of the file. It is never identical twice. The general structure remains recognizable, but small lines shift, branches change direction, and portions occasionally disappear. Researchers who attempted to standardize the mark produced a version that stopped appearing entirely.

One damaged note compares it to a symbol from an old performance tradition, then crosses the comparison out so heavily that only three words remain legible:

████████ IN YELLOW.

No explanation follows. The next page is blank except for a price written in the corner.

Loose Notes

He is not an Overgod.

We are increasingly unsure why that distinction makes us feel better.

The shop may be inaccessible to the gods because it is outside their jurisdiction. "Outside" may be the wrong word.

Do not ask him about the yellow mark. He offered to sell the answer.
