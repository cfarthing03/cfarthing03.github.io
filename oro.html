The Continent of Sin

Oro

City of Innovation

The former Dead Zone transformed into the continent's center of machinery, invention, Automatons, and modern Pride.

At the Table: Arriving in Oro

You do not approach Oro by road.

The city arrives beneath you from the window of an airship or rises ahead through the glass of a train car, and the first impression is movement. Tracks curve across the landscape in impossible lines. Towers bristle with cranes, platforms, gantries, lifts, pipes, and rotating machinery. Airships hang at different altitudes like ships waiting for harbor space, their shadows moving slowly over roofs below.

As you draw closer, the noise becomes physical.

Metal strikes metal in uneven rhythms. Steam vents in sudden white bursts. Chains rattle through pulley systems. Gears turn behind protective grates. Somewhere, something gives a sharp mechanical shriek, followed by several seconds of silence, followed by applause.

The station or docking platform is its own city within the city. Cargo moves in every direction. Automatons carry crates with perfect balance through crowds of travelers. Machinists shout measurements to one another from scaffolds. Porters steer wheeled platforms around exposed cables. A sign warns you not to stand beneath suspended machinery, and directly beneath it someone is standing beneath suspended machinery.

The floor vibrates faintly under your feet.

At first you assume it is the train, then realize the train has stopped.

Oro never entirely stops moving.

Workshops open directly onto the street. Half-built devices sit on tables beside cups of cold coffee and piles of handwritten notes. An Automaton with one arm removed is arguing with a repair technician while using the remaining arm to point at the part they clearly believe is the problem. A young machinist runs past carrying a smoking metal cylinder with both hands while three people behind them shout contradictory instructions.

Nothing looks finished, yet almost nothing looks neglected.

Walls have been opened to run new pipes. Bridges have temporary supports because someone is replacing the old ones. Entire building fronts are covered with chalk measurements. New mechanisms have been bolted beside older mechanisms rather than replacing them completely, creating layers of invention that reveal how many times the city has rebuilt itself.

Even the people seem to move with the assumption that things can be improved.

A shopkeeper adjusts a hinge while speaking to a customer. A child takes apart a toy at the table instead of playing with it. Two engineers stop in the middle of an argument to stare at a stranger's prosthetic limb, exchange one look, then immediately start discussing how they would redesign it.

Above all of this, airships pass slowly between towers.

Their shadows cross the streets like moving clouds.

Oro is not finished.

That is the point.

From the Dead Zone

Oro was built in territory once known as the Dead Zone. Transforming that scar into one of the most advanced cities in the world became part of the city's identity.

Residents are proud of the fact that the place itself is an argument for creation. Something ruined did not have to remain ruined. It could become a workshop, a home, a transit hub, a laboratory, or something nobody had invented yet.

A City Without Roads In

Oro can only be reached by train or airship, both invented in the city. Airships are the more common method and make the sky feel almost like another street system.

Docks and platforms are massive. Cargo moves constantly. Travelers arrive beside machines they may have never seen before. The city controls access naturally because anyone entering must use Oro's infrastructure to do it.

Machinists

Machinists dominate much of Oro's working culture. Entire districts are filled with workshops, factories, testing grounds, repair houses, private inventors, schools, and specialized businesses.

Failure is visible here. Broken prototypes are common. Explosions are not unheard of. A machine that did not work yesterday may be celebrated tomorrow because someone finally discovered why.

Oro respects people who make things, especially people willing to fail publicly until they make something better.

Automatons

Automatons make up a large portion of Oro's population and are ordinary participants in city life. They work, study, travel, own property, build relationships, and occupy social spaces without being treated as curiosities simply for existing.

This can be jarring for visitors from places where Automatons are rare. In Oro, the unusual thing is not seeing one. The unusual thing is reacting as though seeing one should be remarkable.

Technology Without Equal

Among predominantly human cities, Oro is the most technologically advanced place in the world. The dwarven districts of Sol are the major exception, rivaling or surpassing Oro in some fields.

That rivalry is productive. Engineers argue about methods. Inventors compare designs. New technology spreads between the two cultures unevenly, sometimes through cooperation and sometimes because someone saw what the other side built and refused to be outdone.

Modern Pride

Oro reflects the current God of Pride through creation, achievement, identity, craftsmanship, and the satisfaction of making something worth being proud of.

Success is visible here. People put names on inventions. Workshops display accomplishments. Students brag about difficult projects. Failure is tolerated because improvement is admired more than perfection.

The city's version of Pride is not about being better than everyone else. It is about becoming good enough at something that you can look at your own work and know exactly why it matters.
