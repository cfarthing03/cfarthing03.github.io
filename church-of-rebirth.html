Faith Associated with Wrath

Church of Rebirth

A loud, persistent faith built around Ashwing, the ancient Chosen of Wrath who is said to have died and risen again.

Ashwing

The Church of Rebirth does not worship Wrath directly. Its central figure is Ashwing, one of Wrath's ancient Chosen. Ashwing's true name has been lost, but stories about him survived long after the man himself disappeared from history.

Those stories describe a warrior with an impossible connection to the Sun. Depending on who is telling the story, Ashwing controlled it, carried part of it, or simply fought with enough divine power that later generations stopped bothering with the distinction.

Death and Return

The defining story of the church is Ashwing's death. Tradition holds that he was slain and later returned as a phoenix, turning death from an ending into proof that a sufficiently powerful life can begin again.

That belief gives the church its name. Followers do not merely admire survival. They are fascinated by transformation after destruction: the idea that something can burn, end, and still return as something greater than it had been before.

Fire, Ash, and Palin

Palin's ordinary funerary practices already involve cremation, largely because a desert city has practical reasons not to preserve every body indefinitely. The Church of Rebirth gives that custom additional meaning. Fire becomes a symbol of transition, ash becomes evidence that an ending has occurred, and the phoenix becomes the promise that an ending may not be final.

The church is especially visible in Palin through sermons, pamphlets, public preaching, and posters that have a habit of appearing in places where nobody remembers granting permission to put them.

Wrath's Opinion

Wrath has never shown particular hostility toward the Church of Rebirth, but neither has he declared it uniquely correct. Followers receive the same divine benefits available to others who fight in Wrath's name, and the church's more enthusiastic members consider that extremely persuasive evidence.

That interpretation is not universal. Many citizens of Palin treat the church as mostly harmless, occasionally inspiring, and frequently annoying. Its loudest believers take Wrath's neutrality as confirmation. Everyone else tends to take it as Wrath being Wrath.
