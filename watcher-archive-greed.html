The Last Voice

Greed awakened last.

He remembers becoming aware in a small lizard form and immediately recognizing five people he had never met.

Greed // deposition GR-1

I was alive for perhaps six minutes before Envy informed me we were escaping.

I asked from what.

He said, “Him.”

I looked at Pride.

Then I looked at Sloth.

I did not require a second explanation.

The others had waited for him. Envy's plan had been built around the assumption that all six relic-born Sins needed to be awake before they fled. The moment the connection told them Greed was there, the plan moved.

It moved too early for Greed's awakening to finish naturally.

The Escape

The facility did not fail in one dramatic explosion. It failed in dozens of boring ways at exactly the right time.

A lock Pride believed repaired opened on the first pull. An alarm circuit reported a closed door as open and an open door as closed. A reserve conduit had been drained into a dead system. Two corridor clocks disagreed by enough that a scheduled guard rotation overlapped itself. A restraint mechanism jammed with a filing that had been placed inside the housing months earlier.

Then Wrath bent the available time.

Watcher instruments centuries later still detected temporal irregularity in stone removed from the corridor. What occurred inside that window cannot be timed normally. Pride's own emergency log reports events out of order.

ESC-PRIDE-ERR // emergency notessequence unstable

Containment west failed.

W-03 not in assigned chamber.

G-05 reserve output missing.

L-02 moving S-01.

E-04 location unknown.

New asset absent.

Correct sequence impossible. Clocks unreliable.

Watcher analysts believe “new asset” refers to Greed.

Gluttony released energy Pride expected to own. Lust kept the group together through corridors designed to separate them. Sloth, still unable to hold a stable body, had to be physically supported for part of the route. Envy was rarely where anyone expected him to be and apparently exactly where the plan required.

Greed mostly tried not to die.

Greed

I would like the record to show I contributed.

Watcher: What did you contribute?

I bit Pride.

Watcher: Is that true?

It improved morale.

Envy refused to confirm the bite.

GR-OUTSIDE-01First-hours testimony

The First Thing He Owned

Greed's earliest memory outside the laboratory is not money.

It is a stone.

Greed // deposition GR-2

I picked it up because I could.

No inventory number. No permission. No one asking what function it served.

I said, “Mine.”

Lust laughed so hard she nearly dropped Sloth.

I kept the stone for several centuries.

The Watchers cannot verify the stone's current location. Greed claims this proves nothing except that archivists are nosy.

The anecdote matters because it is the earliest surviving expression of Greed's relationship with ownership as something other than domination. Pride's laboratory treated ownership as absolute authority: I made you, therefore you belong to me. Greed's first possession was meaningless, freely chosen, and valuable only because no one could make him pick it up or put it down.

What the Escape Cost Him

The other Sins had time between awakening and escape to develop. Their relic states had progressed into animal forms and, for several, eventually into humanoid forms. Greed was pulled out almost immediately.

Whatever process should have completed his immortality did not.

He could feel the defect before anyone else could measure it.

Greed // deposition GR-3

The others felt continuous.

I did not.

Imagine a contract missing its final page.

You can infer the terms. You can even perform most of them.

But somewhere in the back of your mind is the certainty that the signature isn't there.

For the first time, the group faced a problem Pride had not caused deliberately but his captivity had still created: Greed could die in a way the others might not.

Greed's response was immediate and entirely in character.

He wrote terms.

The First Contract

The earliest known use of Greed's Contract Magic was a contract with himself.

The original document does not survive intact. Five portions of text were recovered from five separate traditions centuries apart, each preserving nearly identical legal structure. The Watchers believe this mirrors the ritual itself.

Greed divided his body into five deliberately separated portions and bound them under one agreement. No single portion would be permitted to constitute his entire life. If the whole failed, the surviving holdings would remain Greed. When the cycle reached its end, the separated parts would be compelled to return to one another and restore common form.

CON-GR-SELF/01 // reconstructed clause, incompleteI, being both maker and subject of these terms, divide ownership of the whole among five holdings, each holding recognized as myself and as custodian of the remainder.

No loss of one holding shall constitute loss of the whole.

No separation shall terminate claim.

At the end of the body's lawful cycle, all remaining holdings shall return to common possession and restore the signatory as a single party.

Duration: until amended by the unanimous consent of myself.

The ritual was not painless. Greed was still newly awakened and physically unstable. Separating five anchor portions from a body that barely knew how to stay one body forced him to perform on himself a controlled version of the division he had just seen inflicted on Sloth.

Greed

I did not enjoy the symmetry.

Sloth stayed with me.

He didn't say much.

He didn't need to.

Watcher annotationThe archive does not identify the five specific body portions. Existing sources agree on the number and legal function, not the anatomy. We are not filling that gap with folklore.

He Drafted It Like a Frightened Person

Later copies make the immortality contract look elegant. The earliest fragments are full of revisions.

Greed crossed out words that could be interpreted too narrowly. He added definitions for whole, loss, separation, and self. He rewrote one clause after Wrath pointed out that “return” did not necessarily mean “return alive.” Envy found a loophole involving simultaneous destruction of multiple holdings. Lust objected to language that allowed a future version of Greed to revoke the contract impulsively. Gluttony asked what happened if one piece was eaten.

Sloth sat beside him through most of it.

Greed

I was terrified.

There. You may file that in enormous letters if you like.

I had just become alive and immediately learned I had done it incorrectly.

Everyone else was helping me write around death like we were fixing a bad lease.

It was absurd.

It worked.

The Watchers consider this one of the clearest moments in the archive where the six cease looking like escaped experiments and begin looking like a group of people building a life together.

Long-cycle observationHilnem records

An Immortality Written Instead of Inherited

Every few thousand years, Greed's constructed immortality reaches the point encoded in that first agreement. His body separates into its component holdings. Greed ceases to be whole without becoming permanently dead. Over time, the pieces draw back together and the contract restores him.

It is not the immortality the other Sins developed naturally.

It is a workaround so audacious it became theology.

Greed

People call it cheating death.

Death was not a party to the agreement.

I prefer “excluded from negotiation.”

The Last One They Waited For

Greed's humor obscures the part of his history he discusses least comfortably: five people suffered longer because they refused to leave before he woke.

Greed // deposition GR-6

I did not know them.

They did not know me.

They waited anyway.

Do you understand how irritatingly difficult that is to repay?

You cannot put a monetary value on it.

I have tried.

That may be the earliest emotional root of Greed's later relationship with value. Worth is not identical to price. A cheap coin can matter because of who gave it. A promise can be more valuable than a fortune. Ownership can mean care as easily as possession.

Pride's laboratory taught Greed what rules look like when one person may rewrite them at any moment.

Greed's answer was a rule that bound even the person who wrote it.

Closing assessment

Six Awakenings

Sloth woke alone and learned to refuse. Lust woke and gave that loneliness an answer. Wrath learned the difference between punishment and justice. Envy learned the machinery well enough to break it without being seen. Gluttony learned that nothing living should be treated as disposable. Greed woke last and inherited a family already in motion.

Envy's plan got them out.

Greed's first contract made sure “out” could become a future instead of a brief delay before death.

They did not become people because Pride designed them well.

They became people in every place his design failed to account for choice.
