The Seven Sins

Wrath

Punishment. Justice. Strength. Courage. Fury with a purpose.

Wrath is not worshipped as mindless anger. He is the force behind punishment when punishment is deserved, the strength to stand your ground, and the fury required to act when restraint has failed.

The God of Wrath

Wrath is the god of punishment, justice, strength, courage, and controlled fury. In the modern world, his name does not mean throwing yourself into every fight you can find. Wrath is the anger that has a reason behind it: the refusal to accept cruelty, the courage to confront someone stronger than you, and the willingness to punish those who knowingly cause harm.

His most common divine image is a massive white beast with six enormous palms holding the Sun. The image is less about savagery than power under control. Wrath is terrifying because he can destroy, but his followers care far more about whether that destruction has a purpose.

Before Godhood

Wrath did not begin as a person. His earliest known form was a simple obsidian blade. When the weapon bonded with a worthy host, it became frighteningly effective at finding and exploiting even the smallest weakness in an opponent. As the bond deepened, the host gained greater control over the weapon and the transformations it could produce.

That power had a cost. A host who pushed the bond too far could lose control entirely, allowing the weapon's power to erupt through the body until it tore them limb from limb. Wrath's history has always carried that same warning: strength without control eventually turns on the person wielding it.

The Ultimate Warrior

Wrath's ascension into divinity was tied to an agreement with Greed, but one of the events that defined him afterward was his battle with Pride. Wrath's command over time made him nearly impossible to defeat. Wounds could be undone, attacks avoided before they landed, and mistakes erased almost as soon as they were made.

The confrontation cemented the title The Ultimate Warrior. It also shaped the way many people understand Wrath today. The point is not that he always wins because he is the strongest creature in the room. The point is that preparation, discipline, and complete commitment can turn strength into something far more dangerous.

Palin and the Blessing of Wrath

Palin is the city most strongly shaped by Wrath. Combat there is religion, tradition, competition, conflict resolution, and, unusually, a source of literal sustenance. Those who fight in Wrath's name while within the city receive his blessing and no longer require food or water. Leave Palin and the blessing disappears; return, and it can be restored.

That has made fighting inseparable from everyday life. Brawls, duels, arenas, and training are not signs that Palin is collapsing into violence. They are part of how the culture functions. What matters is whether the fight has purpose, whether the opponent is worthy, and whether the person throwing the punch can still remember why they started.
