The Seven Virtues

Patience

Endurance, waiting, forgiveness, and the strength to survive what cannot be hurried.

Patience is the most overtly celestial of the Virtues: a blinding, many-winged god whose presence feels less like a person entering a room and more like heaven opening above it.

The Virtue of Waiting

Patience does not teach passivity. His ideal is the ability to endure pain, uncertainty, anger, and fear without letting the moment decide who you become. To wait is to refuse to be ruled by panic.

From Eustace to Patience

Before his godhood, Patience was known by other names, including Eustace and Abram. He was slain in the final battle, but his soul did not simply vanish. In death he went on to rescue his comrades and raise them into divinity, creating the opposing pantheon now known as the Seven Virtues.

A God Almost Too Angelic

Modern depictions push his divinity to excess: layered wings, impossible halos, radiant eyes, veils and masks, and enough light to make looking directly at him uncomfortable. The design is deliberate. Patience should feel holy in a way that is beautiful, severe, and slightly frightening.

When Patience Becomes Control

The danger in Patience is easy to miss. Endurance can become obedience. Forgiveness can become pressure to accept what should not be accepted. Waiting can become an excuse for never acting at all. His faith lives in that uncomfortable space between perseverance and surrender.
