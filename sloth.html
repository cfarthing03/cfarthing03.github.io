The Seven Sins

Sloth

Rest, recovery, death, rebirth, and the patience to survive what cannot be rushed.

Sloth is associated with sleep and rest, but reducing him to laziness misses almost everything that matters. His domain is the rhythm between exhaustion and recovery, life and death, ending and beginning again.

The God of Rest

Sloth represents sleep, recovery, death, rebirth, and natural cycles. Rest is not treated as weakness in his worship. It is the thing that allows effort to continue. Death is not always framed as failure either; sometimes an ending is part of the process that makes another beginning possible.

That understanding did not come from comfort. Of all the Sins, Sloth's divine identity may have been shaped most directly by suffering.

Seventy-Five Years

Sloth was one of the first Sins to awaken and one of the first to defy Pride. For that defiance, he was subjected to seventy-five years of torment. His body was disassembled, experimented on, and repeatedly pushed beyond what should have killed him. Sleep, recovery, and even death were denied to him.

Two substances emerged from that suffering. The first was Life's Blood, a bright red material associated with vitality, new beginnings, and rebirth. The second was a dark, viscous liquid formed from the remnants and weight of what had been done to him. Sloth's deteriorating body still leaks the black substance.

A Body Held Together

Much of Sloth's power is spent simply keeping his own body intact. He survived what was done to him, but survival did not mean returning to the person he had been beforehand. That distinction matters deeply to his followers.

Recovery is not the same thing as erasing the wound. A person can heal and still be changed. Sloth's existence is proof of both sides of that truth.

Sol, the Sleeping City

Sol built its culture around the rhythms associated with Sloth. The city sleeps through much of the day and wakes at night, filling the darkness with festivals, celebrations, and communal life. Rest is scheduled into the culture rather than treated as something people earn only after exhausting themselves.

The result is a city with a remarkably simple philosophy: tomorrow will still be there. Probably.
