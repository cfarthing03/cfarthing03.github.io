Religion of Wrath

The Gladiators

Strength, discipline, justice, and combat treated as an act of worship.

Strength With a Purpose

The Gladiators are the primary religious order devoted to Wrath. To them, strength is not valuable simply because it allows someone to dominate another person. Strength matters because it gives a person the ability to protect, endure, confront injustice, and act when someone weaker cannot.

Combat is therefore one of their clearest forms of worship. A fight can be prayer, training, judgment, celebration, or a lesson. What matters is not violence for its own sake, but the purpose behind it.

A Worthy Opponent

The Gladiators place great importance on the idea of a worthy fight. Defeating someone who cannot meaningfully defend themselves proves very little, and deliberately seeking helpless opponents is viewed as dishonorable. A difficult battle against someone capable of testing you is far more spiritually meaningful than an effortless victory.

Victory is celebrated, but defeat is not automatically shameful. A hard loss can expose weaknesses, teach discipline, and make someone stronger. For many Gladiators, a defeat that changes you is worth more than ten victories that taught you nothing.

Justice and Protection

Wrath's connection to punishment and justice also shapes the order. Gladiators frequently place themselves between the vulnerable and those who would exploit them, and some serve as guards, enforcers, duelists, or protectors. Anger is not something they are expected to suppress completely. They are expected to control it well enough to aim it at the right target.

The ideal Gladiator is not the person who never becomes furious. It is the person who can still decide what to do with that fury.

The Arenas of Palin

Arenas in Palin are more than entertainment venues. They are places of worship, competition, ceremony, training, and public life. Wrath's blessing also gives combat a practical role in survival: those who fight in his name while within Palin can be sustained without food or water.

As a result, fighting is woven into both religion and daily culture. The Gladiators simply take that relationship more seriously than most.
