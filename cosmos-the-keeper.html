Person of Note

Cosmos the Keeper

A daughter of Greed, one of his five separated pieces, and the keeper of a record that preserves history as it actually happened rather than as anyone wishes it had.

The Keeper of All Knowledge

Cosmos is the daughter of Greed and one of the five pieces into which he was separated. Her purpose is preservation. Events do not need to be glorious, public, or flattering to be kept. If they happened, they belong to the record.

That makes Cosmos something far more dangerous than a simple historian. Nations can rewrite their victories. Religions can polish their saints. Survivors can turn shame into legend. Cosmos preserves what actually occurred.

The Library

Greed created a small pocket dimension for her known simply as the Library. Within it, history is stored without national propaganda, religious revision, or the distortions that gather around a story after enough generations have repeated it.

The Library is valuable precisely because it does not care which version of an event became popular. Its purpose is not to protect reputations. Its purpose is to remember.

Knowledge Without Prophecy

Cosmos cannot see the future. She does not possess prophecy, nor does the Library contain events that have not yet happened.

What she does possess is an enormous record of what people have already done. By comparing habits, choices, failures, and patterns across history, Cosmos can make predictions with frightening accuracy. She does not need to know the future when the past repeats itself so reliably.

The Contract of Noninterference

Her contract with Greed limits how freely she may interfere in mortal affairs. Except in circumstances severe enough to threaten the world itself, Cosmos observes and records rather than acts.

The result is a keeper who may understand exactly how a disaster began, recognize the pattern it is following, and still be bound to watch it unfold. Knowledge does not automatically grant permission to change what comes next.
