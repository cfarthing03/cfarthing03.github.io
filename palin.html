The Continent of Sin

Palin

City of Warriors

Wrath's desert city, where combat is worship, civic ritual, justice, and sometimes the price of citizenship.

At the Table: Arriving in Palin

Your players do not see a normal city skyline. They see a sea of enormous collapsible homes spread across the desert, each one shaped more like an elaborate movable pavilion or oversized teepee than a permanent building. Some are richly decorated, reinforced, divided into multiple rooms, and large enough to house entire families. Smoke rises from temporary cooking areas. Training grounds appear between clusters of homes. Somewhere in the distance, people are almost certainly fighting.

The only structure that truly dominates the horizon is the Colosseum. Everything else can move. Palin feels less like a city built on the desert and more like a city that has learned how to travel across it without ever ceasing to be itself.

The City of Warriors

Palin is the city most strongly associated with Wrath. Combat is woven into daily life, but the city is not a collection of mindless brawlers. Fighting is religion, training, entertainment, conflict resolution, social ritual, and a test of character.

Strength matters, but control matters just as much. A warrior who cannot stop themselves is considered dangerous rather than impressive.

A City Designed to Move

Nearly every home in Palin is built to be taken apart, transported, and rebuilt somewhere else. Calling them teepees is useful as a visual reference, but undersells them badly. Many are enormous, comfortable structures with multiple living spaces, storage, decoration, reinforced frames, and materials designed to survive repeated assembly.

The mobility solves several problems. Families relocate for food, water, and sanitation. They move when nearby land becomes inconvenient. They move because they are bored of a location. Most importantly, they move because fights happen, and a large brawl can damage everything around it.

A collapsible home is easier to relocate before trouble arrives and easier to replace if trouble arrives anyway. The result is a city whose neighborhoods constantly shift. Directions in Palin can become outdated surprisingly quickly.

The Colosseum

The Colosseum is the one great structure in Palin that does not move. It is the city's religious, civic, and cultural center. Public contests, major disputes, formal challenges, ceremonies, and important celebrations all pass through it.

Combat there is dangerous, but not designed around casual death. Staff intervene when fighters approach the point where a contest is likely to become fatal. Victory is supposed to prove something. A corpse usually proves very little.

Citizenship Must Be Earned

Citizenship in Palin requires arena combat. It is not one possible route. It is the route. Anyone who wants to become a citizen must prove themselves through the Colosseum.

The test is not simply about winning through brute force. Palin respects endurance, courage, judgment, discipline, and the willingness to stand your ground.

Wrath's Blessing

Those who fight in Wrath's name while within Palin can receive his blessing, allowing them to survive without normal food or water. In a desert city, this is not a symbolic miracle. It is one of the reasons Palin can function at the scale it does.
