TRANSCRIPTION WITHHELD

God Tongue

The marks do not look written. They look remembered by the surface.

✣◊⟁✣◬

ARCHIVAL NOTE / ORIGIN UNKNOWN

A language no one remembers inventing.

Every culture with a surviving account gives it a different name. God Tongue is simply the most common translation.

Tradition claims the gods speak it naturally and use it to invoke their magic. No surviving mortal source can prove that claim. No scholar has identified an ancestor language, a geographic origin, or a period in which the script first appeared.

It simply enters history already old.

Several inscriptions predate the structures that contain them. In at least two cases, excavation records claim the writing was found on stone surfaces that had never been exposed to tools.

OBSERVATION / WRITTEN EXPOSURE

Reading is enough.

The text does not need to be spoken to produce an effect.

Readers describe pressure behind the eyes, a metallic taste, and the sensation that the room has become subtly too small. Lamps dim. Loose objects tremble. Ink runs sideways across pages despite level surfaces.

During one documented reading, every door in the building opened at once.

During another, witnesses reported a sound beneath the floor like stone grinding against stone. The structure stood on open sand.

For this reason, surviving copies are rarely reproduced in full. Most scholarly records replace original glyphs with numbered placeholders.

OBSERVATION / VOCALIZATION

A mortal body is not built to say it.

The danger increases dramatically when God Tongue is spoken aloud.

Witnesses consistently report an immediate loss of strength followed by bleeding from the eyes, nose, mouth, or ears. The speaker's voice may continue after their lungs should no longer be capable of producing the sound.

One word is survivable for some.

Two has killed people who believed themselves prepared.

The injury does not resemble ordinary exhaustion. Accounts describe something being spent that rest, food, and healing cannot quickly replace. “Life force” is imprecise, but no better term has endured.

WITNESS STATEMENT / SOURCE DAMAGED

“He spoke once and the candles went out. He spoke again and I forgot, for a moment, that the sky was supposed to be above us.”

DISPUTED RECORD / SEVEN WORDS

The longest mortal utterance.

The most persistent account concerns a mortal who spoke seven words in succession.

Not a sentence.

Seven names.

The true names of the gods.

No complete transcription survives. Copies that claim to contain even one of the names contradict one another, and several are obvious frauds. The oldest record does not write the words at all. It leaves seven deliberate gaps.

The speaker's identity is missing from the same document.

So is the final page.

LATE MARGINALIA / HAND UNKNOWN

The seven names appear to be older than the titles mortals use now. Later traditions preserve echoes of them in stories of devils, tempters, and the vices given flesh, though every surviving list disagrees on spelling and order.

A name has value. A true name has more. Priests forget. Contracts do not. If you insist on looking further, ask the one who still keeps accounts.

MARGIN NOTE / HAND UNKNOWN

Do not confuse ignorance with safety.

We do not know whether God Tongue commands the world, persuades it, reminds it of older rules, or merely tears holes through whatever laws normally keep reality consistent.

We do know the world reacts.

That is enough.
