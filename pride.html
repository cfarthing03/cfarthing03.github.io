The Seven Sins

Pride

Self-worth, identity, achievement, creation, and the confidence to recognize what you have become.

The current God of Pride is not the same being as the malevolent spirit that previously carried the title. That distinction is essential to understanding what Pride means in the modern world.

The Current God of Pride

The modern God of Pride represents self-worth, confidence, achievement, identity, and personal excellence. Pride does not require believing yourself superior to everyone else. It can be as simple as recognizing that you did something difficult, that your work matters, or that part of who you are deserves to be celebrated rather than hidden.

That interpretation has made Pride especially important to people and communities whose identities have historically been treated as something to diminish. Pride in culture, heritage, identity, craft, and personal growth all fall comfortably within the god's modern domain.

The Lion

The lion is Pride's primary symbol. It represents confidence, strength, leadership, and presence, but also grace. The image is meant to communicate someone who does not need to make everyone around them smaller in order to stand tall.

That distinction separates the current god from the being that once carried the same title.

The Previous Pride

The former Pride was an immortal and malevolent spirit, not simply an earlier version of the current deity. Long ago, the Old Six imprisoned him. He eventually returned by manipulating 922, a grief-stricken warforged who had once been part of the very group responsible for his imprisonment.

922 had spent a lifetime trying to restore a lost companion. After losing her, his grief curdled into the belief that mortality was humanity's greatest weakness and that human beings only destroyed what they touched. Pride exploited that belief and pursued the extermination of humanity in favor of supposedly flawless Clockwork beings.

That old ideology treated improvement as replacement: if something was flawed, discard it and build something better. The current God of Pride rejects that premise. Improvement can mean growth, recognition, confidence, and creation without requiring anyone else to disappear.

Oro

Oro is a city of craftsmanship, invention, achievement, and creation. It grew around the shattered forge where the original Relics of Sin were first made, tying the city to some of the oldest history surrounding the pantheon.

People in Oro are encouraged to take pride in what they create and what they become. Achievement is meant to be celebrated without turning someone else's success into a threat. The modern city's understanding of Pride is not about standing above everyone else. It is about standing firmly enough that you no longer need to.
