Why This File Is Different

The Watchers do not maintain a second full biography of Pride here. Too much of Pride's history already dominates the public archive, and repeating it would bury the smaller questions researchers, priests, travelers, and frankly nosy adventurers keep asking us to verify.

This file therefore catalogs corrections, commonly confused facts, and current divine preferences that are easy to lose inside larger histories.

Watcher annotationWhen discussing the laboratory period, “Pride” refers to the original malevolent Pride who created and experimented on the relic-born Sins. When discussing modern tastes, offerings, worship, or sacred traditions, the entry refers to the current gods unless explicitly noted otherwise. The current Pride is not the original Pride.

Frequently Requested Historical Clarifications

Was Pride the first Sin to awaken?

No. Pride existed before the relic-born Sins, but he does not count in their awakening order because he was originally human rather than one of the relics. Among the relic-born Sins, Sloth was first.

What did the original Pride think the relics were?

Tools. He created powerful artifacts to control humanity. He did not expect consciousness and never appears to have imagined the artifacts could become people.

Did consciousness come from Pride's experiments?

No evidence supports that conclusion. Sloth's awakening surprised Pride precisely because it was unrelated to the experiment being conducted at the time.

Did Pride ever treat the Sins kindly?

Yes. Surviving testimony describes kindness being used as reward, leverage, false safety, or a way to make later compliance easier. Kind behavior did not reflect recognition of their personhood.

Could Pride actually kill them?

Pride believed he could. The Sins believed he could. He never permanently killed one of them, so the claim was never conclusively tested.

Why was Sloth punished so much more severely?

Sloth's refusal was the first open rejection of Pride's intended control. Pride responded with seventy-five years of “correction.” Later Sins lived with Sloth's condition as proof of what Pride might do if pushed far enough.

Why create the Rixel Virus?

Sloth demonstrated that a useful person could simply refuse to cooperate. Pride wanted a contingency that could force obedience even when consent was absent. He ordered Lust to make it.

Was Sloth Extract intentional?

No. Pride damaged Sloth until his body began deteriorating, discovered that the resulting material had violent effects, and experimented on it. The discovery was incidental; the weaponization came afterward.

Was Life's Blood meant to help humanity?

No. Its beneficial effects made distribution easier. Dependency made users controllable, and the embedded kill switch eventually allowed Pride and Dr. Rayabond to kill roughly sixty-five percent of humanity during the Clockwork Resurgence.

Did Pride know why the Vile Mark percentage matches the Life's Blood death toll?

No verified record indicates that he did. The matching sixty-five-percent figures remain unexplained. Sloth may have an idea about why the prevalence of Vile Marks mirrors the Life's Blood death toll, but he has never claimed certainty and has not provided the Watchers with a complete explanation.

Is the current God of Pride the same being as the original Pride in these laboratory records?

No. This distinction is non-negotiable in Watcher cataloguing. The current Pride and the previous malevolent Pride are not the same person. Do not transfer the crimes, motives, or psychology of one onto the other because they occupy the same divine title.

What Offerings Do the Sins Actually Like?

Offerings do not need to be expensive. Intent matters more than spectacle. A person who gives something meaningful is generally better received than a person trying to impress a god with waste.

WrathFighting performed with purpose, earned glory, bringing someone genuinely deserving to justice, or tea. Yes, tea. The archive has enough independent confirmations that this is not a joke.
EnvyBooks, knowledge, a discovery, a record of something poorly understood, or information on a newly encountered creature or species.
GreedAnything with monetary value. The value does not need to be high. A cheap coin freely given can be a perfectly acceptable offering; the point is that something recognized as value is being exchanged.
SlothCelebrations. A party held in his honor, people relaxing together, or simply allowing him to watch others enjoy themselves. He appears to value the enjoyment more than any object placed on an altar.
GluttonyFood remains the obvious and reliable choice. Shared food is generally viewed more favorably than wasteful excess.
LustJewelry, fine art, handmade work, or anything that clearly required time and care. Objects used to show affection fit her better than expensive objects selected only because they are expensive.
PrideNew inventions, designs, prototypes, unusual ideas, clever improvements, and things someone created because they were proud enough of the idea to make it real.

How Do the Sins Feel About Their Worshipers?

They care.

Watcher records do not support the cynical idea that worshipers are simply mana, political influence, or disposable followers. The Sins regard their worshipers as vital and care for them deeply, even when individual gods express that care very differently.

Wrath may challenge. Envy may advise indirectly. Greed may turn the relationship into obligations and promises. Sloth may simply be present. Lust is openly relational. Gluttony tends toward hospitality and family. Pride encourages making, improving, and becoming.

The styles differ. The underlying attachment does not.

Do the Sins Have Holy Days?

Not really.

There are regional festivals, temple events, local anniversaries, and celebrations associated with individual faiths, but there is no universal calendar of official Sin holidays comparable to what some organized religions maintain.

Sloth has the strongest festival tradition. Celebrations attributed to him are common precisely because celebration, rest, and people enjoying one another's company fit his domains. They are better understood as festivals than mandatory holy days.

What Do the Sins Think About Sacrifice?

Deliberately killing an innocent person or animal as an offering is broadly regarded by the Sins as grotesque and unacceptable.

Wrath is the closest thing to an exception, and even that requires precision. A warrior dying in battle, accepting a lethal risk in a just cause, or giving their life while actively fighting for something can be honorable in Wrath's eyes. That is not the same thing as dragging a helpless person or animal to an altar and killing them “for Wrath.” He does not consider ritual murder honorable because someone says his name while doing it.

Watcher practical guidanceIf your planned offering requires a kidnapped baby, a tied animal, or someone begging not to die, you have misunderstood the pantheon and should stop immediately.

The Most Important Historical Correction

The original Pride's records describe the other Sins as creations, tools, assets, and subjects. That language has survived in enough later scholarship that it can quietly distort the story.

Pride created the relics.

He did not create their choices.

He did not plan Sloth's refusal, Lust's loyalty to him, Wrath's sense of justice, Envy's conspiracy, Gluttony's devotion to family, or Greed's decision to write immortality for himself.

The Watchers therefore treat consciousness as the point at which Pride's ownership claim stops being useful historical language. Whatever he believed they were, they became people capable of wanting something else.
