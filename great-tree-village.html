The Great Tree Village

A settlement built around a tree that should not exist here, hidden within a forest where the border between worlds has never fully healed.

The Sprout

During the era of Overgod's Revenge, a mysterious portal opened in a relatively uninhabited stretch of forest. The portal did not remain, but something was left behind: a single tree sprout.

The sprout grew with impossible speed. As its roots deepened and its canopy widened, small rifts began opening around it into the Feywild. Strange plants appeared first. Then unfamiliar animals. Eventually entire pockets of foreign ecology began spilling through and taking root in the surrounding forest.

The settlement that formed around the tree became known simply as the Great Tree Village.

The Kitsune

The Kitsune who came through those rifts regard the Great Tree as sacred. Most remain close to it for their entire lives, and leaving the village is uncommon enough that encountering a Kitsune elsewhere in the world is itself unusual.

The village does not advertise its existence. Old texts occasionally mention a hidden settlement beneath an impossible tree, but most people dismiss those accounts as folklore.

Some of those same texts speak about another people who arrived through the rifts: YOKAI. Whatever they are, the oldest surviving records become strangely incomplete whenever the subject appears.

The Great Hunt

There was a time when outsiders discovered that some Kitsune could perceive things other people could not. Those creatures exerted influence on the world whether ordinary people could see them or not, but Kitsune eyes offered a way to make the invisible visible.

What followed became known within the village as The Great Hunt.

Kitsune were hunted for their eyes. Their bodies became resources. Their ability to see what others could not became something outsiders believed could be harvested, stolen, and used.

By the time the violence ended, the number of Kitsune capable of that sight had fallen dramatically. The village closed itself to visitors, and secrecy became a matter of survival rather than tradition.

The Council's Duty

The Council of the Great Tree eventually declared that every remaining Kitsune possessing the sight would serve the village directly.

This is not a request.

A person who refuses the duty, or anyone who knowingly conceals someone who possesses the ability, may be sentenced to death.

No public account explains what service to the Council actually requires. The people who perform it rarely discuss their work. Villagers learn not to ask about the bruises, the cuts, or why some return home staring through conversations as though part of them never came back.

Fifteen

Children are not told the full history.

Until the age of fifteen, they may hear fragments from older relatives: warnings without context, names spoken too quietly, stories that stop when someone younger enters the room. The truth is withheld until their rite of passage into adulthood.

Part of that rite determines whether the child possesses the sight. The other part teaches them what happened during the Great Hunt, why the village closed itself away, and why those who can see must serve.

By the time the rite is finished, most do not question whether the duty is fair. They have been taught that questioning it is the same as questioning whether their people deserve to survive.
