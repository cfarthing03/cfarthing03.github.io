Tales Character

Odin

A player character remembered through Clockwork Resurgence and Overgod's Revenge.

Known History

Odin's earliest memory is that of when his parents gave him to his grandparents and left. His grandparents were upper members of the Church of Lust’s fourth largest temple. Through them, he learned the ways of Lust and practiced his faith. While at the temple he grew up with another child of the church Sister Nadia. Every day they would practice their faith and try their best to spread Lust’s message of peace and love, until one day when they were 12. Odin and Nadia had gone out to grab food before their test of faith and on the way back Odin got a glance into the window of a home. Inside was a father murdering his family, Odin watch in complete terror not being able to move. At that moment Odin’s faith began to break. Nadia eventually dragged him by the arm back to the temple, luckily for her she did not see the horrors through the window. When they took their test of faith Aldred Hihwar came to their temple and after passing Nadia and touching Odin’s head told him that he was a danger to the church and to leave at once. His grandparents who expected him to pass with flying colors disowned him on the spot. He never really talked to them after that. Nadia was taken to parts of the temple that Odin couldn’t access due to just being a common member of the church. As the days and the years passed by his faith left more and more. He barely got to see Nadia, and when he did she wasn’t that joyful girl he knew in his childhood who would talk to him to the point that he would want to tape her mouth shut, instead she was cold and ignored him. This all built up inside him for years until one night he heard his grandparents talking to Nadia about how she was what they wanted Odin to be. They left the temple and started living at another much smaller one, where he has stayed for the last three months.

It's Odin again!

Legacy

Odin, The Overgod

Odin's legacy extends far beyond any single nation.

At some point following his adventures, Odin obtained the powers of an Overgod, granting him influence over the world on a scale few beings could ever hope to achieve.

Rather than retreating from mortal affairs, Odin began using this power to confront what he saw as the atrocities and injustices of the world. However, he quickly discovered that simply destroying threats as they appeared would never be enough.

Instead, Odin began manipulating events themselves.

Wars, alliances, discoveries, and seemingly insignificant encounters were subtly influenced over generations, all pieces of a plan whose full scope could only be understood by Odin himself.

At the center of this plan was Pride.

Knowing that Pride would eventually return, Odin dedicated enormous effort toward ensuring that when that moment came, Pride would return not as an enemy of the world, but once again as one of the Sins.

Entire generations lived and died without ever realizing that certain events within their lives had been nudged into place by the hand of an Overgod preparing for something centuries in the future.

Odin therefore became something far stranger than a hero.

He became one of the hidden architects of history.

Odin, The Penitent Overgod

Odin's ending would become one of the more tragic among the heroes who survived.

When an Overgod was slain and the continued stability of the realm demanded that someone take their place, Odin was faced with an impossible choice.

He chose his wife.

She became the new Overgod, condemned to an existence of endless suffering as she holds the realm together in the place of the being Odin destroyed.

Odin has never forgiven himself for that decision.

Much of his remaining life is spent beside her, visiting whenever he can and enduring what little of her torment he is capable of sharing. Together, they wait for the possibility that one day another being may be able to take her place and finally release her from the burden Odin placed upon her.

Yet Odin's influence on the divine world did not end there.

At some point through his actions, Odin inadvertently brought about the existence of an entirely new pantheon known as Odin's Children.

Very little is currently understood about them. It is believed that there are ten Children in total, though most have yet to reveal themselves to the wider world. Their identities, purposes, and even their morality remain uncertain. Some may prove benevolent, others malicious, and it is entirely possible that several do not fit comfortably into either category.

Odin himself never intended to become the father of a pantheon, and exactly how much control or responsibility he holds over his Children remains unclear.

For now, they exist as another strange consequence of the enormous influence Odin has had upon the divine order of the world.

When Odin is not with his wife, he spends his time with his daughter or traveling the world, quietly checking upon mortals, the Sins, and occasionally the strange divine offspring that now bear his name. He works to ensure that the fragile stability they fought so hard to achieve does not collapse.

He does not consider any of this heroism.

To Odin, it is atonement.

He knows that eventually his own life will end, whether through age, battle, or sacrifice. Until that day comes, he has chosen to spend what remains of it protecting the world, watching over what he helped create, and remaining beside the woman whose suffering guarantees that world continues to exist.

Odin saved the world.

Now he spends the rest of his life living with the cost of how he did it.
