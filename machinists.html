Religion of Pride

The Machinists

Creation, purpose, invention, progress, and the belief that an idea deserves the chance to become real.

The Faith of Makers

The Machinists are the primary religious order devoted to Pride. A large portion of the faith is made up of sentient Automatons, though human inventors, engineers, craftspeople, and other creators are common as well. The two groups often approach Pride for different reasons, but both center their worship on creation, purpose, and the belief that making something new is one of the clearest ways a person can leave a mark on the world.

For the Machinists, creation is not limited to machines. An invention, a new technique, an unusual design, or even a better way of solving an old problem can all be expressions of Pride.

Automatons and Purpose

Many sentient Automatons praise Pride for giving them life, identity, and purpose. Their faith is deeply personal because the act of creation is inseparable from their own existence. At the same time, the Machinists reject the idea that being created for one purpose means an Automaton must remain trapped in it forever.

A being may have been built for a task, but sentience gives them the right to decide what they become next. Pride is therefore worshipped not only as the force behind creation, but as the confidence to take ownership of the life that creation produced.

Human Inventors

Human Machinists tend to focus more heavily on invention and progress. They see Pride as a patron of creativity, craftsmanship, experimentation, and the stubborn drive to make something that did not exist before.

Failed inventions are rarely treated as shameful. A workshop full of broken prototypes can be just as meaningful as a completed masterpiece if every failure taught the creator something useful.

Keeping Inspiration Alive

One of the order's most important beliefs is that inspiration should be protected. Machinists often describe Pride as the force that keeps an idea alive long enough for someone to finally build it. A creator might spend years chasing a design that everyone else considers impossible, impractical, or ridiculous.

To the Machinists, refusing to abandon that spark can itself be worship.

The Power Crystals

Among the greatest mysteries connected to Automatons are the Power Crystals. Roughly a dozen different varieties are known, and each possesses its own strange, otherworldly power. No one knows where the crystals originated, who created them, or where they naturally exist.

For an Automaton, a Power Crystal is not simply a useful component. It is the core that keeps them active. The crystal is slotted into the chest and provides the energy required for the Automaton to function. If the crystal is removed and no replacement is present, the Automaton immediately falls limp and remains inactive until a Power Crystal is placed back into the core.

Crystals and Ability

The Power Crystal also determines what extraordinary abilities an Automaton can access. Different crystals grant different powers, meaning the same Automaton may be capable of entirely different things depending on which crystal is currently slotted into their chest.

This ability to change does not make the core unimportant. Quite the opposite. For many Automatons, choosing, protecting, inheriting, or replacing a Power Crystal can carry enormous personal meaning.

Why the Crystals Are Attributed to Pride

There is no proof that Pride created the Power Crystals. Even the Machinists cannot explain where they came from. Automatons nevertheless frequently attribute the crystals to him because they are the objects that quite literally allow many of them to live, and Pride is the god they associate with life through creation, identity, purpose, and becoming something more than what was originally built.

Human Machinists are often just as fascinated, though usually for a different reason. To an inventor, the Power Crystals represent one of the greatest unanswered problems in existence: a family of unknown materials capable of sustaining sentient machines and granting radically different powers with no known origin. Studying them can become scientific work and religious devotion at the same time.

Temples That Look Like Workshops

Machinist temples often resemble workshops more than traditional places of worship. Forges, drafting tables, mechanical components, half-built machines, prototypes, and strange inventions are common sights. Prayer may take the form of designing, repairing, constructing, or teaching someone else how to create.

The order reflects the current Pride's rejection of superiority. Creating something extraordinary does not prove that its creator is better than everyone else. It proves that they made something worth being proud of. Creation is meant to expand what is possible, not replace everyone who came before.
