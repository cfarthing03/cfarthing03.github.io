The Seven Virtues

Temperance

Balance, restraint, proportion, and the power to stop before desire becomes command.

Temperance is a severe celestial god framed by eclipses, orbiting spheres, and impossible geometry: moderation expressed as cosmic control.

The Middle Path

Temperance is not simple abstinence. His virtue is proportion: eat, but do not be ruled by hunger; celebrate, but know when celebration ends; wield power, but recognize when enough has become too much.

A Controlled Heaven

His divine imagery leans toward eclipses, floating spheres, perfectly measured rings, and suspended debris. Everything appears moments from catastrophe, yet every object remains exactly where it belongs.

Ritual Restraint

Followers often turn limits into ritual. Portions, schedules, periods of silence, measured drink, controlled expenditure, and deliberate pauses can all become acts of worship. The point is not deprivation for its own sake, but proof that appetite does not make the final decision.

When Balance Becomes Suppression

Temperance becomes dangerous when moderation is imposed from outside. Emotion can be labeled excess. Pleasure can become suspect. Individual needs can be flattened into whatever amount an authority has declared reasonable. Perfect balance is easy when no one is allowed to move.
