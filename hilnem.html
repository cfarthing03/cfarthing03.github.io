The Continent of Sin

Hilnem

Greed's Ever-Lasting Contract

The continent's great commercial center, protected and imprisoned by the same system of divine contracts.

At the Table: Entering Hilnem

The first thing your players notice is how deliberate everything feels. The road into Hilnem is broad, clean, and almost aggressively maintained. Gatehouses rise on either side of the entrance, not like fortifications built to keep people out, but like enormous legal thresholds. Brass plaques, carved stone notices, hanging signs, and glowing contract script cover the entryway. None of it is hidden. Hilnem wants visitors to know exactly what they are agreeing to before they step across the line.

Once inside, the city opens into avenues lined with banks, merchant halls, counting houses, private offices, public markets, contract brokers, jewelers, warehouses, luxury homes, and old family estates. There is wealth everywhere, but almost none of it feels chaotic. Even the busiest districts move with a practiced rhythm. Porters know where to stand. Caravans know where to unload. Buyers know which windows handle disputes, appraisals, escrow, insurance, or contract review. Conversations that would be casual anywhere else have a strange precision here. People repeat numbers back to one another. They confirm names. They ask exactly what a promise means before accepting it.

Hilnem does not feel oppressive in the way a fortress does. It feels like a city where everything has already been considered. That can be comforting right up until someone realizes the city has considered them too.

A City Built on Magical Contracts

Magical contracts are the foundation of Hilnem's society. They are used for business agreements, property transfers, employment, loans, partnerships, services, insurance, inheritance, construction, security, private agreements, and thousands of smaller arrangements that residents barely think about anymore. A person in Hilnem may sign more magically binding documents in a month than someone elsewhere sees in a lifetime.

The magic itself is simple in principle. Two or more parties knowingly accept a set of terms, and the contract enforces the agreement. The danger is that magic does not care whether the agreement was wise. It does not care whether one person read every clause, understood every consequence, or underestimated how literal the wording would become. It enforces what was agreed to.

Because of that, contract literacy is part of ordinary education. Children are taught to recognize common clauses. Adults learn never to verbally agree to something they have not heard completely. Wealthy families retain personal contract specialists. Businesses advertise that their agreements have been reviewed by trusted legal houses. Some people make entire careers finding loopholes in contracts that are technically perfect but practically disastrous.

The City Itself Is an Agreement

The most important magical contract in Hilnem is the one nobody physically signs. By entering the city, a person agrees to its civic laws. The terms are posted at major entrances and can be reviewed before entry. Once someone crosses into Hilnem, ignorance stops being a defense.

The same principle continues at a smaller scale throughout the city. A tavern may bind guests to refrain from violence while inside. A jeweler may require anyone entering a private showroom to agree not to steal, damage, or magically alter merchandise. A bank vault may bind employees to confidentiality. A private home may establish rules for guests before the door opens.

Visitors sometimes find this exhausting. Residents barely notice. To someone born in Hilnem, asking what a doorway means before walking through it is no stranger than checking whether a door says pull or push.

Why Hilnem Is So Safe

Hilnem's reputation for safety is not propaganda. Violent crime is comparatively rare, theft is difficult, fraud is dangerous, and openly breaking agreements is one of the fastest ways to ruin a life. A person can carry a small fortune across certain districts with far less fear than they would in Hus.

That does not mean Hilnem is harmless. The danger simply changes shape. The city is filled with people who understand contracts better than their victims. A merchant can destroy someone without drawing a blade. A poorly negotiated loan can become a prison built entirely from legal wording. An employer can technically fulfill every obligation while making an employee miserable. Hilnem is safest when everyone understands the rules, and most dangerous when one person understands them much better than the other.

The locals know this. They are polite, cautious, and often frustratingly specific. To outsiders it can seem cold. To Hilnem residents, it is basic self-defense.

Commerce as Culture

Hilnem is not merely a place where trade happens. Trade is part of its identity. Markets are enormous, specialized, and intensely organized. Entire streets are dedicated to particular goods. One district may be filled with gemstones, metalwork, fine clothing, and enchanted luxury goods, while another is dominated by bulk grain, lumber, livestock, ore, and industrial materials.

Deals are public entertainment in some circles. Wealthy merchants host dinners where negotiations last for hours. Families build reputations over generations. A clever agreement can be admired the way a duel might be admired in Palin or a performance in Etro. People tell stories about legendary trades, disastrous loopholes, and contracts so elegant that even rival firms grudgingly praise them.

Money is important, but ownership matters just as much. Hilnem respects the idea that if you earned something, built something, bought something, or were given something under valid terms, it is yours. The city takes that concept very seriously.

Greed's Presence

Greed currently resides within Hilnem, and that changes the atmosphere of the city in subtle ways. His presence is not constant spectacle. Most citizens do not wake each morning expecting to see a god walking past the market. But everyone knows he is there.

That knowledge affects politics, religion, commerce, and social status. Major institutions care deeply about Greed's opinion. Old families tell stories about direct dealings with him. Contract specialists study the kinds of agreements associated with his influence. Ambitious citizens sometimes arrive in Hilnem hoping that if they become important enough, wealthy enough, clever enough, or useful enough, Greed might notice.

He does not need to rule from a throne for the city to feel like his. Hilnem's entire culture already speaks his language.

How Hilnem Feels to Live In

Daily life in Hilnem is comfortable if a person understands the system. Streets are maintained. Public services are reliable. Businesses are predictable. People generally know what they are responsible for and what they are owed in return. There is a deep sense that society functions because obligations are real.

The downside is that spontaneity can feel dangerous. Casual promises carry weight. Trust is often formalized. People may ask for written terms where someone from another city would expect a handshake. For some residents, this creates security. For others, it makes every relationship feel slightly transactional.

Hilnem is a city where ambition is welcome, wealth is visible, and success is respected. The catch is simple: know exactly what you agreed to before you reach for it.
