The Continent of Sin

Hilnem

Greed's Ever-Lasting Contract

The continent's great commercial center, protected and imprisoned by the same system of divine contracts.

At the Table: Entering Hilnem

Your players notice the rules before they notice the wealth. Signs at gates, plaques beside doors, posted terms in shops, and carefully worded notices make it clear that Hilnem expects people to understand exactly what they are agreeing to. Merchants speak with practiced precision. Clerks ask for confirmation before accepting anything important. Even casual transactions can feel strangely formal.

Then the scale of the city settles in. Trade flows constantly through Hilnem. Caravans, vaults, markets, private firms, magical services, contract specialists, and wealthy families all operate within a system built around enforceable agreements. It is one of the richest places on the continent, but what makes it memorable is how orderly that wealth feels.

A City Built on Magical Contracts

Hilnem is the commercial center of the Continent of Sin, and magical contracts are woven into nearly every part of its society. These agreements can enforce terms accepted by the people involved and apply consequences written directly into the contract. Some citizens can create them naturally, while others rely on trained specialists, businesses, and families known for contract work.

The magic does not decide whether an agreement is kind, fair, or sensible. It only enforces what was accepted. That makes careful wording a survival skill. A poorly written magical contract can be technically valid and still ruin someone's life.

The City Itself Is an Agreement

Entering Hilnem means accepting its civic rules. The city operates on the assumption that visitors have agreed to abide by its laws simply by choosing to enter. Individual businesses and buildings can add their own terms, and some places make those conditions obvious before a person crosses the threshold.

This can make Hilnem feel intimidating at first. Locals barely notice. They grow up understanding that a door may carry terms, a service may include conditions, and a promise may be more than social etiquette. Reading before agreeing is as ordinary here as checking the price before buying something anywhere else.

Why Hilnem Is So Safe

Hilnem's strictness produces one of its strangest benefits. It is one of the safest major cities on the continent. Theft, fraud, violence, and other crimes are difficult to sustain when businesses, buildings, services, and civic systems can bind people to clear rules with magical consequences.

That safety is not the same thing as freedom from danger. Hilnem is full of people who know exactly how to use rules to their advantage. Lawyers, contract writers, merchants, negotiators, and wealthy families can be more dangerous than a street criminal because what they are doing may be completely legal.

Greed's City

Hilnem reflects Greed through ownership, exchange, ambition, and the value placed on keeping what has been earned. Wanting more is not considered shameful here. It is expected. The important question is whether a person is prepared to understand the cost of getting it.

Greed currently resides within the city, making Hilnem one of the rare places where a god's presence is not merely represented through temples, symbols, and clergy. His influence is part of daily political and commercial life.
