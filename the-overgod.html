The Overgod

Some truths about the gods are dangerous. Others are dangerous because they reveal that the gods are not the highest thing in the room.

What an Overgod Is

Gods rule concepts, cities, faiths, and the people who place their belief in them. An Overgod exists above that order. It is not merely a stronger god. It is the authority responsible for the universe itself, the being that creates it, maintains it, and decides what reality is permitted to remain.

The universe governed by an Overgod resides within its chest. This is not a symbol used by priests or a metaphor invented by scholars. It is the closest surviving description of the relationship between the being and the reality it carries.

You keep calling it a body because you need it to have one.

The Strings

The power most often associated with an Overgod is described as Strings. To those capable of perceiving them, the world appears bound together by threads that can be reached for, twisted, pulled, or shattered. A sufficiently powerful hand can alter what a thing is, what it was, whether it continues, or whether it ever had the right to exist at all.

The Strings are not literal objects. They are a visualization of the will required to take responsibility for an existence, or for its absence, and change it. They are invisible to almost everyone because, in ordinary reality, there is nothing there to see. They exist when reality needs them to exist, or when the one reaching for them needs a shape through which to exert that will.

To see a String, then, is not simply to possess better sight. It is to possess enough authority over reality that reality gives your intention something to hold.

Closer. Still not quite.

The Creator of This World

The Overgod created the world recorded throughout this archive. The same being stands behind all three great arcs of its history. What changes from age to age is not his involvement, but his conclusion about what has gone wrong and how much of his creation must be sacrificed to correct it.

First Intervention

Tales of the Old Six

At first, the Overgod did not seek the destruction of humanity. He tried to preserve the beauty of his creation by containing what he considered its most disgusting qualities in one place. The cycle was deliberate. The suffering was, by his reasoning, compartmentalized. The rest of creation could remain untouched while everything he rejected was kept where it belonged.

The people inside that system resisted. The Old Six broke the cycle he had so carefully arranged.

The Overgod did not immediately erase them for it. He allowed the damaged order to remain and watched.

Allowed? Is that what you think happened?
Second Intervention

Clockwork Resurgence

By the time of Clockwork, his conclusion had changed. The flaw was no longer a collection of ugly qualities that could be contained. The flaw was humanity itself.

His answer was Pride. Once again, Pride would become the instrument through which the world was corrected. An army of machines would wipe humanity away and give the Overgod the chance to begin again, freeing both himself and the rest of his creation from the shackles mortals had made for them.

Humanity resisted again. From his perspective, they resisted the cure while continuing to prove the disease.

They always called survival virtue when it inconvenienced me.
Final Intervention

The Overgod's Revenge

By the third great conflict, containment and replacement had both failed. The Overgod activated a contingency intended to end the argument entirely: the Horsemen. Their purpose was not conquest. Their purpose was deletion. The universe would be wiped clean so another could be made from scratch.

He did not intend for everyone to vanish with it. Seven mortals were selected from among those who had experienced humanity's horrors firsthand. Each was given a special gift and the promise of a place in what came next, provided they helped destroy what remained of the old world. They were called The Virtues, long before any of them became gods.

The Virtues succeeded in summoning the Four Horsemen. The party stopped the end of reality and, this time, reached the Overgod himself.

The Death That Wasn't

The common version says the Overgod was killed.

The Overgod was killed.No.

What actually happened was separation. He was cut off from their reality. The distinction matters because an Overgod is not merely a resident of the universe who can be removed without consequence. The universe depended on the office he occupied. Severing him from it created a vacancy that reality could not survive for long.

And this, according to the surviving record, was ultimately what he wanted.

Recovered interpretation

The Overgod had spent ages trying to force the world back into a shape he recognized as his. By the end, he knew it no longer belonged to him in any meaningful sense. He could not win without destroying the thing he wanted to control. So he arranged a final exit that would preserve the world while ensuring that the people who defeated him inherited the cost of keeping it alive.

Now ask yourself who wrote that interpretation.

Odin's Choice

The vacancy required another Overgod. Odin could not simply take the position himself. He was an illborn Overgod, a being who obtained Overgod power artificially rather than being born with it. He could wield that power, but he could not become the true anchor of this reality.

Two viable choices remained within his family. His daughter, a half-born Overgod, born mortal and Overgod from the beginning, could take the burden. So could his mortal wife because of her Chronomancy.

The Overgod left Odin with the decision.

Which life would he destroy to keep every other life in existence?

Odin chose his wife.

She now holds reality together in constant agony. Her mortal body cannot naturally sustain what she has become, so Chronomancy keeps her alive as she is torn apart and reconstructed again and again. The process does not end. It cannot end while she remains the thing standing between the universe and collapse.

Odin lives with the knowledge that he made that choice.

The former Overgod delights in it.

Why He Did Any of It

Calling the Overgod cruel is easy. Calling him protective is also easy. Both descriptions become inadequate at the scale on which he thinks. He created a universe and regarded himself as responsible for everything inside it. That responsibility became ownership. Ownership became control. Every rebellion against his design became further evidence that the design needed correction.

He did not believe himself to be destroying his creation for pleasure. He believed he was pruning, containing, replacing, and finally resetting something that had escaped the purpose for which he made it.

That does not make the suffering smaller.

And yet you still need me to be the villain. How comforting.
