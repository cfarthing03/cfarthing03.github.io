The Third Voice

By the time Wrath awakened, Sloth and Lust had already established the pattern. Wrath's first animal form is consistently described as a lion in his representative coloration. His first recorded reaction was not wonder. It was suspicion.

He recognized Sloth and Lust as his own kind immediately. That recognition carried no names, no memories, and no explanation. It was simply certainty. Three awakenings were enough for the Sins to begin suspecting the remaining relics might eventually wake as well.

Wrath // deposition W-2

I knew them before I knew Pride.

Not who they were. What they were.

Mine isn't the right word.

Family wasn't the word yet either.

Just: not him.

Pride noticed Wrath's power quickly. More importantly, he noticed that Wrath and Sloth could interfere with one another in ways that made either one harder to control alone.

He turned that into policy.

Two Leashes

Pride made Wrath and Sloth responsible for stopping one another.

When Sloth resisted, Wrath was ordered to counter him. When Wrath pushed beyond instruction, Sloth was used to restrain or disrupt him. Pride then made sure each believed the other had chosen the role.

The Watchers recovered duplicate versions of several orders. The copy shown to Wrath says Sloth “requested stronger containment.” The copy shown to Sloth says Wrath “volunteered to enforce correction.” The master copy says neither.

PAIR-W/S-31 // message comparisonthree surviving variants

Master: Deploy W-03 if S-01 refuses chamber entry.

Version delivered to W-03: S-01 has become unstable and requested intervention if he cannot comply voluntarily.

Version delivered to S-01: W-03 has asked to oversee today's correction personally.

Watcher determination: deliberate conflict engineering, confidence 99%.
Wrath

I thought he wanted to hold me down.

I thought every time Sloth looked afraid of me, it proved Pride was right.

Fear looks a lot like guilt if someone tells you what you're seeing first.

The hatred lasted. So did the fear beneath it.

Wrath // later answer concerning Sloth

I hated him because Pride needed me to.

I hated myself because some of it became real.

Those are also different things.

The First Time Pride Made Them Fight

The earliest forced confrontation between Wrath and Sloth survives in three accounts that only make sense when read together.

Wrath remembers Pride telling him Sloth had become dangerous and needed to be stopped before he injured Lust. Sloth remembers Pride telling him Wrath wanted to prove himself by forcing Sloth back into compliance. Pride's master log describes neither story. It records a control trial.

PAIR-W/S-06 // control trialauthorship: Pride

Objective: determine whether W-03 can interrupt S-01 resistance state and whether S-01 can suppress W-03 escalation.

Both subjects entered under separate instruction.

Do not reveal opposing instruction set unless cooperation develops.

Result: mutual hostility increased. Control efficacy acceptable.

Watcher note: “mutual hostility increased” is the only emotional observation in a four-page technical report.

Wrath attacked first.

He remembers the room narrowing around the certainty that he was preventing something worse. Sloth tried to get past him rather than strike him. Pride called that evasive aggression. Wrath believed him. When Sloth finally fought back, Pride called it confirmation.

Wrath // deposition W-4

I broke his jaw.

He looked at me like I had betrayed him.

I remember being angry at the look.

That is the part that makes me sick now.

I thought, “Don't act surprised. You asked for this.”

He hadn't.

Sloth's corresponding file records that he spent most of the encounter trying to say Lust was safe. His jaw was too damaged to form the sentence clearly.

Pride ended the trial only after both Sins had demonstrated that each could contain the other.

Then he praised Wrath for protecting everyone.

Borrowed Time

Wrath's greatest laboratory value was not combat. It was time.

Pride had experiments that required waiting: growth, decay, adaptation, repeated exposure, recovery, dependency. Wrath allowed waiting to become optional. Chambers were placed under temporal distortion so processes could advance far beyond the amount of ordinary time Pride was willing to spend.

The ratios vary too widely to reconstruct a single standard. Some records imply hours outside became days within. Others describe research cycles that should have required years being completed before surrounding equipment had gathered dust.

TEMP-CHAMBER 8 // PrideW-03 maintained acceleration beyond prior tolerance. Subject objected to continued use after tremor onset and loss of focus. Informed subject that interruption would invalidate six months of internal results.

Subject resumed.

Wrath hated wasted effort. Pride learned to use that against him. If Wrath stopped, every creature or material inside the chamber might lose an experiment's worth of meaning. Pride framed endurance as responsibility.

Wrath eventually recognized the trick.

Wrath

He'd put the cruelty in front of me and call stopping it wasteful.

Very efficient.

I respect efficiency.

I despise him for making me respect the sentence.

Correction

Wrath rebelled more than once. He was never punished as catastrophically as Sloth, but the difference should not be mistaken for mercy.

Pride had already discovered that Sloth's fate was useful as a threat. Wrath knew there was a line somewhere between “pain Pride considers sufficient” and seventy-five years. He did not know where it was.

Several correction logs involve temporal repetition. A single injury could be reset and inflicted again before the body retained permanent damage. The point was not medical experimentation. The point was demonstrating that Pride could make one moment last as long as he wanted.

CORR-W03 / iteration testReset successful. Subject retains memory across reversal.

Repeated fracture produced increasing anticipatory response despite absence of cumulative structural damage.

At iteration 23, subject offered compliance before instruction was repeated.
Wrath // deposition W-8

People hear “reset” and imagine it didn't happen.

It happened.

Then it unhappened to the bone.

Not to me.

That distinction became foundational to Wrath's later thinking. An injury erased is not the same as an injustice undone. Consequences are not the only measure of wrongdoing.

The Line He Would Not Cross

Wrath wanted to fight Pride. He also knew exactly what open defiance had done to Sloth.

His surviving statements are full of shame about restraint. Watcher analysts do not share his interpretation. Pride had designed the environment so that courage had a body count. A dramatic rebellion could have resulted in someone else being placed on the table.

Wrath

I kept waiting until I was sure.

Then I hated myself for waiting.

Then I'd see Sloth and wait again.

I thought bravery meant not being afraid.

Turns out fear can tell you who else is standing behind you.

What Pride called cowardice, Wrath eventually reframed as judgment: strength used without considering who pays for it is only another form of domination.

The Rules He Made for Himself

Wrath could not control Pride's rules, so he started making smaller ones that Pride did not get to write.

Marks found scratched into the inside of one holding chamber appear to correspond to a personal code later described in Wrath's testimony. The wording changed over time, but the purpose did not: create a boundary inside himself that Pride could not move.

  • Do not strike someone who cannot strike back unless they are actively harming another person.
  • Do not punish because you are angry; decide first, then act.
  • If Pride says someone “deserves” pain, ask what they did.
  • If the answer is “disobedience,” that is not an answer.
  • Fear is not permission to make someone else pay for it.
Wrath

I broke my own rules plenty.

That's why I wrote them down again.

A rule isn't proof you're good.

It's something to notice when you're becoming worse.

Watcher researchers consider these marks the earliest surviving evidence that Wrath's justice was not born fully formed. He built it deliberately, often after failing to live up to it.

He Kept the Word “Correction”

Pride used correction to mean pain imposed until behavior matched his preference.

Wrath kept the word and rebuilt it.

In later doctrine, correction requires a wrong that can be named, judgment proportionate to the wrong, and enough strength to make the judgment meaningful. Punishment without justice is not correction. It is cruelty wearing procedure as a uniform.

Wrath

He taught me punishment first.

Justice took longer.

I'm glad it did.

Fast lessons were usually his.

Personhood note

The Person Behind the Anger

Wrath's archive contains shouting, broken equipment, formal challenges, and at least one Pride note complaining that W-03 had become “obsessively literal about fairness.” It also contains evidence of a person who needed rules because the man controlling his life changed the rules whenever obedience required it.

Wrath likes clean answers but learned to live with complicated ones. He can hate what he did to Sloth and still understand he was manipulated. He can believe punishment is necessary and still despise punishment performed for pleasure. He can be furious and careful at the same time.

The Watchers consider that contradiction more useful than any account that reduces him to rage.
