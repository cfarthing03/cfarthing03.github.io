The First Voice

Pride did not create the Relics of Sin to become people. Every surviving note from the period treats them as instruments: powerful artifacts designed to control humanity, each useful because it could do something ordinary magic could not. Consciousness was not an intended feature. There is no evidence Pride even considered it possible.

Then Sloth spoke.

The earliest reconstruction places him still almost entirely within his relic-state: aware, cold, unable to understand the room around him, and staring up at the man who had made him. Pride's notes change tone for only a few lines. The handwriting becomes larger. Several measurements are repeated. One sentence is underlined three times.

Recovered laboratory fragment // attributed to PrideArtifact responded without command phrase. Response was not echo, stored instruction, sympathetic resonance, or projected thought. It demonstrated preference. It asked where it was. It asked what I was.

This outcome is unrelated to the procedure being conducted. Repeat observation before drawing conclusions.

Sloth later described the moment with considerably fewer words.

Statement attributed to Sloth // transcription copied by Watcher field team

Cold.

Then awake.

He looked scared.

I thought that meant he wouldn't hurt me.

Watcher annotationSloth remained the only conscious relic long enough to believe he might be the only one forever. Later Sins describe awakening with an immediate recognition that previously awakened relics were “like them.” Sloth had no such precedent.

Useful Again

Pride's first response to Sloth's consciousness was fascination. His second was utility. Samples were removed from the relic and tested in dozens of applications. Pride attempted to reproduce Sloth's properties in other materials, isolate individual functions, and manufacture smaller tools from harvested fragments. The early trials repeatedly failed.

The records do not show Pride asking whether removal hurt. They show him measuring how much material could be taken before performance declined.

Pride // experimental asset S-01Sentience appears persistent despite material reduction. Subject complains of fatigue after extraction. No corresponding loss in output beyond expected depletion.

Conclusion: complaint is behavioral, not mechanically relevant.

During this period Sloth's awareness grew faster than his ability to act. He moved from artifact to a small animal-like body—a purple sheep in every surviving description—before eventually gaining enough power to take the humanoid form later associated with him. Pride's language never changed with him. Artifact became subject. Subject became asset. It never became person.

Several decades of notes contain brief intervals in which Pride appears almost gentle: food placed within reach, conversation, praise after successful tests, a hand resting on wool rather than holding it down. Later testimony from every surviving Sin describes the same pattern. Kindness arrived when compliance weakened and disappeared when it stopped working.

Then There Were Two

Lust's awakening changed Sloth more than Pride initially understood. She recognized him immediately as something like herself. Sloth, after years of assuming no one else would ever wake, finally had another person who understood what it meant to exist as one of Pride's tools.

Their earliest friendship is poorly documented because Pride considered their private conversations irrelevant. That omission may have been his first serious mistake.

Sloth

I thought I was the mistake.

Then she woke up.

Pink bird. Loud. Asked too many questions.

After that I knew I wasn't alone.

At approximately the same period, Sloth overheard enough of Pride's planning to understand the intended future of the relics. Pride had not built them to protect humanity. He had built them to make humanity controllable, and Sloth himself would eventually be used to hurt people who refused that control.

Sloth refused.

Watcher analysts consider Lust's presence the decisive variable. Sloth had endured extraction and experimentation for years while believing resistance would only leave him alone with Pride. Friendship gave him something Pride had never planned for: a reason to choose a side other than his creator's.

Seventy-Five Years

Pride called what followed correction.

The correction lasted seventy-five years.

Sloth's own memories are almost unusably sparse. He remembers being tired. He remembers cold tables. He remembers waking when he believed he had already been awake. He remembers the word “again.” He remembers Pride asking whether he was ready to cooperate and the relief of realizing he could still answer no.

Sloth

Hurt.

Slept sometimes. I think.

He kept putting me back.

He'd ask again.

I kept saying no.

Lust's surviving account is less merciful. She describes Pride opening Sloth's body to learn where a relic ended and a person began, separating limbs and sections of tissue, suspending pieces in vessels while the remaining body continued to move, and then forcing the pieces back together to see whether Sloth would recover. When recovery became reliable, Pride stopped treating disassembly as a risk.

Later procedures were performed faster. Pride already knew Sloth would survive.

Lust // copied from a Watcher interview, source age uncertain

He screamed until there wasn't enough throat left to make the sound. Pride wrote that down.

He pulled him apart, waited, put him together, and asked him the same question.

Sometimes he was kind afterward. That was worse. Sloth would look at him like maybe this time it was finished.

It was never finished.

Pride // correction cycle 412Subject continues refusal despite escalating loss of structural integrity. Compliance response remains absent.

Unexpected benefit: repeated reconstitution has altered recovered material. Detached matter no longer fully expires. It attempts return even after separation from primary mass.

Retain samples.

The final decades ceased to resemble the correction's beginning. Sloth's body no longer remembered a single stable shape. Flesh sloughed away, liquefied, and crawled back toward him. Portions fused where they should not and had to be torn free before they could be placed correctly. Eventually there was no “correctly.” Sloth spent increasing amounts of power simply keeping a recognizable body around himself.

That condition persists.

Sloth Extract

Sloth Extract was not the objective of the torture. Pride discovered it because the torture had damaged Sloth so severely that his deteriorating body began producing material with properties Pride had never designed.

He experimented because it was there.

Pride // material derivative S-EIngestion produces catastrophic structural convergence. Musculature contracts beyond intended range. Skeletal geometry follows force rather than anatomical limitation. Convulsive motion continues after major fracture.

Not suitable for controlled deployment. Potential weapon value remains considerable.

Watcher medical reconstructions are consistent across every recovered exposure: the body appears to fold inward against itself. Muscles seize hard enough to drag joints out of alignment. Limbs torque toward the torso. Ribs and long bones fracture under forces generated by the victim's own body. The movements resemble a seizure only from a distance; internally, the pattern is closer to an entire body attempting to occupy less space than its skeleton permits.

High exposure is rapidly fatal. Lower exposure can leave a survivor with extensive fractures, internal tearing, and permanent deformity.

There is no evidence Pride personally deployed Sloth Extract as a weapon. Humans later recovered it. Surviving records indicate that even those groups considered the substance too indiscriminate and horrifying for routine use.

Watcher annotationThe distinction matters. Pride did not torture Sloth in order to produce Extract. He tortured Sloth for disobedience, noticed what the torture had created, and only then began experimenting on the byproduct.

Life's Blood

Life's Blood came later. Pride refined material harvested from Sloth—blood, regenerative matter, and the altered substance produced by his deteriorating body—until the result no longer looked like a weapon.

It looked like a miracle.

Sold as a drug across the continent, Life's Blood pushed users toward the apparent peak of human capability. Strength increased. Thought sharpened. Reflexes improved. Fatigue receded. Users consistently described the experience as extraordinary.

Then it wore off.

The first dose might sustain a person for a year. Continued use shortened the interval. A year became six months, then a month, then days, then hours. Once dependence established itself, stopping was not withdrawal in the ordinary sense. The user's body had learned to rely on the substance to continue functioning. Without another dose, systems began shutting down until the body died.

Pride // derivative stabilization notesDependency is not a flaw. Dependency is the mechanism.

A population that believes the substance improves them will distribute the control system voluntarily.

The ultimate purpose was extermination. During the Clockwork Resurgence, Pride and Dr. Rayabond activated the embedded kill switch. Approximately sixty-five percent of humanity died.

The Watchers have no language that makes that number smaller.

Recorded Life's Blood mortalityApproximately 65% of humanity died when the kill switch was activated.
Modern Vile Mark prevalenceApproximately 65% of the current population bears what is now called a Vile Mark, granting a unique ability shaped by the bearer's personality.

The matching figures were initially dismissed as coincidence. The surviving Sins do not dismiss them.

Watcher annotation // unresolvedPride died before the connection between Life's Blood survivors, their descendants, and Vile Marks became apparent. The Sins recognize that a connection exists. Sloth is the only one believed to have a working theory, and even he has never claimed certainty. No Watcher source currently establishes the mechanism.
