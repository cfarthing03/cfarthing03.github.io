The First Voice

The Watchers' reconstruction begins with an object inventory, not a birth record. Pride's handwriting lists mass, temperature, mana response, material loss from prior sampling, and the status of a procedure that appears to have had nothing to do with consciousness. Halfway down the page, the format breaks.

There is no triumphant note announcing a successful creation of life. There is a blot of ink, three repeated measurements, and then a sentence written so hard the nib tore the paper.

REC-P/S01-01 // laboratory foliochain of custody: verified

Artifact vocalized without command phrase.

Not echo. Not stored instruction. Not sympathetic response. It demonstrated preference and self-reference. It asked why the room was cold. It asked whether I was the one who made it.

Repeated stimulus produced irritation.

It told me to stop repeating myself.

This outcome is unrelated to the active procedure.

Margin note, same hand: “Do not anthropomorphize an unexpected behavior.”

Sloth remembers the same event without measurements.

Sloth // deposition 4A // answer given after 41 seconds of silence

Cold table.

Bright light.

Couldn't move right.

He was staring.

I thought he looked scared.

I was glad.

Thought scared people were careful.

He had no language for what he was. He did not know there were others. He did not know that the sensation he would later identify as the connection between the Sins was missing because there was no one else awake to answer it. For years, Sloth believed consciousness had happened to him alone.

Watcher character note: In every interview, Sloth describes his first years with less anger than the researchers expected. The recurring emotion is loneliness. When asked when he first understood Pride was dangerous, he answered, “Before I understood he was cruel. Took longer.”

Measured as Material

Pride did not stop experimenting because the relic spoke. He revised the paperwork.

The earliest pages say artifact. Later pages say subject. When Sloth became capable of taking a small animal-like form—a purple sheep, consistently described across every independent source—the notes begin using mobile asset. When he eventually acquired a humanoid shape, Pride wrote morphological improvement.

Nowhere in the surviving lab archive does the word person appear.

REC-P/S01-19 // extraction seriesRemoved material continues to retain sympathetic response to primary body. Subject reports pain during separation and fatigue afterward. No meaningful decrease in long-term function observed.

Operational conclusion: reported discomfort does not correlate with unacceptable performance loss. Continue.

Watcher forensic analysts reconstructed the routine from 113 partially surviving sheets. Pride would remove a measured portion, isolate it, expose it to spellwork or alchemical reagents, test whether its properties persisted, and then return what remained. If the sample failed, he took another. If Sloth protested, Pride recorded agitation. If Sloth became too weak to hold shape, the session ended only until he recovered enough for the next one.

  • Sampling intervals shortened whenever Sloth's recovery time improved.
  • Pride marked pain responses only when they interfered with the procedure.
  • Three records note that Sloth attempted to pull away before restraints were applied. The response column reads: “anticipatory behavior increasing.”
  • A later inventory lists warm blankets among “compliance aids,” beside restraints and sedatives.

The blankets matter because Sloth remembers them.

Sloth // deposition 5C

He'd hurt me.

Then sometimes he'd bring something warm.

Tell me I did well.

First time I thought maybe he was sorry.

Second time too.

More than twice, probably.

I was stupid.

Watcher annotationInterviewing Watchers objected to Sloth's use of “stupid.” He refused correction. His explanation: “Knowing something now doesn't mean I knew it then.” This phrasing is retained because it captures the central problem in the archive: Pride understood enough about hope to use it before Sloth understood enough about manipulation to defend himself.

Then Someone Answered

Lust awakened second. The Watchers deliberately omit her account from this file; her testimony is catalogued under SIN-02. Sloth's description is preserved here because, for him, her awakening is not primarily the beginning of Lust's story. It is the end of his certainty that he would always be alone.

Sloth // deposition 7B

Pink bird.

Wouldn't stop talking.

Good talking.

Asked my name. I didn't have one I liked yet.

Asked what I wanted to be called anyway.

That was new.

Pride asked what I could do.

She asked what I liked.

Sloth says the recognition was immediate. Not memory. Not familiarity. Something underneath both. The instant Lust was awake, he knew she was the same kind of impossible thing he was. He could not explain how.

The friendship that followed is barely visible in Pride's records. Two “assets” spent longer than expected in the same chamber. Lust was relocated after “excessive verbal stimulation.” Sloth's compliance dipped when she was removed and improved when she was returned. Pride interpreted the pattern as leverage.

Sloth interpreted it as friendship.

That distinction becomes important when he learned what Pride intended to do with him.

The First Refusal

Sloth overheard enough of a planning session to understand that the relics were not being prepared to protect humanity. Pride had built them because ordinary people were difficult to control. A city could resist an army. A leader could refuse a bribe. A family could hide. A person could say no.

Pride wanted tools that made refusal irrelevant.

When ordered to participate, Sloth said no.

REC-P/S01-44 // behavioral correction authorizationauthorship: Pride

Asset S-01 refused direct task after repeated instruction.

Clarified command. Refusal persisted.

Removed secondary asset from room. Refusal persisted.

Offered restoration period, reduced sampling schedule, and private quarters contingent on compliance. Refusal persisted.

Conclusion: sentience is producing a false concept of autonomy. Correction required before deployment.

The word “autonomy” is underlined twice.
Sloth // deposition 9A

He kept making the offer nicer.

I almost said yes.

Not because I wanted to hurt anyone.

Because I was tired.

Then I thought: if I say yes, who's tired after me?

So. No.

The correction authorization is dated. The final termination note is also dated. Between them are seventy-five years.

Seventy-Five Years

Pride called it correction because torture would have required admitting there was someone present to torture.

The first years were methodical. Pride restrained Sloth in humanoid form and mapped exactly how much could be removed while consciousness remained. An arm was separated at the shoulder and placed within Sloth's sightline. The fingers continued to curl when the primary body struggled. Pride noted sympathetic motor response and waited before reattachment.

Later, the separation became smaller and more deliberate. Muscle was peeled away in bands to determine whether it would crawl back toward the body. Sections of skin and relic-flesh were pinned flat beneath glass to watch the edges twitch. Bone was exposed, scored, broken, aligned incorrectly, allowed to begin healing, and broken again so Pride could compare the difference between natural and forced reconstruction.

He opened Sloth's torso often enough that the procedure stops receiving a title in later logs. It becomes a number.

CORR-S01 / cycle 2,118 // PridePrimary body remained conscious after thoracic separation. Vocal response ceased at 03:11 after damage to upper airway. Ocular tracking continued. Repaired airway at 04:02. First vocalization following restoration was refusal.

Increase interval before next vocal restoration. Silence may improve receptivity.

The Watchers cannot prove whether Pride wrote that last sentence as a joke.

Sloth // deposition 11D

Couldn't scream.

Still hurt.

That part surprised me.

Thought if I couldn't make the noise maybe it'd be less.

Wasn't.

He'd lean close to see if I was awake.

I learned not to close my eyes when he asked.

When simple disassembly stopped producing new information, Pride began leaving pieces separated for longer periods. Sloth describes the sensation as being “in too many places and none of them right.” A hand sealed in a jar still hurt when Pride pressed a blade into the palm. A section of shoulder tissue spasmed when the rest of him tried to move. The body remembered itself even when Pride refused to let it be whole.

Sloth // same deposition

Worst wasn't cutting.

Worst was waiting.

You can wait for pain if you know when it starts.

Hard to wait when it already started and you don't know when he puts you back.

Over decades, Pride stopped restraining Sloth before every procedure. Not because Sloth consented. Because there were periods where he no longer had enough intact structure to run.

The Kind Days

The records become hardest to read when the violence pauses.

Pride occasionally restored Sloth completely. He cleaned the table. He allowed him to sleep. He returned clothing. He spoke softly. Once, after a sequence of procedures that left Sloth unable to maintain a face for several days, Pride spent an evening sitting beside him and discussing nothing related to work.

The next morning Pride asked again whether Sloth was ready to cooperate.

Sloth // deposition 13F

I liked those days.

Still do, in memory.

Hate that.

He could be funny.

He could talk like I mattered.

Then he'd ask.

If I said no, he'd get quiet.

Not angry. Quiet.

Angry would've been easier.

Watcher behavioral specialists classify this as coercive intermittent reward: enough care to preserve hope, never enough to change the underlying condition. Sloth did not endure seventy-five uninterrupted years of visible cruelty. He endured cruelty punctuated by carefully placed evidence that maybe the cruelty had ended.

That distinction is one reason his testimony remains so angry at himself and so strangely restrained toward Pride. The manipulation worked often enough to leave good memories inside a bad life.

When His Body Stopped Staying Together

At some point during the correction, Sloth's body ceased reconstructing cleanly.

The earliest warning appears in a Pride note complaining that restored tissue was “losing boundary discipline.” Sloth's forearm would knit back into place, then soften at the seam hours later. Fingers fused, separated, and sagged. Skin gathered like wax warmed too long. Sections of his body began sliding away from the shape he was trying to hold and then pulling themselves back in slow, exhausted strands.

Pride initially treated the change as damage to be corrected. Then he noticed the material that fell from Sloth remained active.

MAT-S01-DECAY // PrideMaterial shed from subject retains unusually aggressive transformative response after separation. Collection must occur before reintegration. Subject becomes agitated when shed matter is removed. No evidence agitation improves yield.

Unexpected result has practical potential.
Sloth // deposition 16A

I couldn't keep my hand.

Not lost it. Keep it.

Would make fingers, they'd soften, run together.

I'd pull them apart.

He watched.

Then started putting bowls under me.

The phrase “putting bowls under me” appears twice in the transcript because the first interviewing Watcher assumed it had been mistranscribed. It had not.

The damage never fully reversed. Sloth escaped with his body still failing to respect its own boundaries. The humanoid form he had once learned to maintain became impossible to hold. Even now, portions of him continuously slump, drip, or peel away before drawing themselves back into the whole. The monstrous appearance associated with modern Sloth is not his natural end-state. It is scar tissue on the scale of an entire body.

This is the origin of what later records call Sloth Extract.

Sloth Extract

Pride did not set out to create Sloth Extract. He damaged Sloth until Sloth's body began producing it, then did what he always did with an unexpected result: he tested it.

The raw material proved monstrously effective when ingested. It did not simply poison the body. It altered the body's relationship with its own shape. Muscles contracted in conflicting directions. The abdomen pulled inward. Shoulders rotated toward the sternum. Hips twisted. Limbs tried to fold against the torso while the skeleton resisted.

Medical descriptions compare the visible motion to a seizure only because observers lacked a better word. Autopsy records tell a worse story: fractures formed from forces generated by the victim's own muscles. Ribs snapped inward. Long bones broke where opposing contractions bent them past tolerance. Joints dislocated and then continued to pull. In severe exposure, the body reduced itself into a compacted posture it had no anatomy to survive.

MED-EX/S01-08 // later human exposuresource: sealed civic archive

Subject remained conscious through initial contraction phase.

Mandible fractured while attempting to speak.

Both humeri fractured without external impact.

Respiration ceased after thoracic compression.

Material was thereafter classified “unsuitable for field deployment.”

Watcher note: This record postdates Pride's original laboratory. Pride is not known to have deployed the raw Extract himself.

Humans eventually obtained it. Even they found it too horrific to use with any regularity.

Watcher annotationThe distinction is essential. The Extract was not a prototype Pride intentionally designed. It was an accidental byproduct of Sloth's prolonged bodily destruction. Pride's culpability lies in recognizing that byproduct as a weapon and continuing the research.

Life's Blood

Life's Blood is what happens when the same line of research becomes elegant.

Using Sloth's harvested blood and the knowledge gained from the Extract, Pride refined the violence into something people would willingly take. Life's Blood made a person feel extraordinary: stronger, faster, sharper, more capable, more alive. The first dose could last long enough that dependence seemed absurd. Then the interval shortened.

Early useA dose may remain effective for months or longer. Users describe clarity, energy, strength, and euphoria.
ProgressionThe required interval contracts: months become weeks, weeks become days, days become hours.
WithdrawalThe body does not simply feel ill. Systems begin shutting down. Long-term users cannot safely stop.
Control mechanismDependency makes continued access equivalent to survival. Distribution therefore becomes leverage.

The final cruelty was not addiction. It was the kill switch.

During the Clockwork Resurgence, Pride and Dr. Rayabond activated the embedded mechanism. Anyone sufficiently altered by Life's Blood died essentially at once. Entire households stopped moving. Streets filled with people who had been healthy seconds before. The best surviving demographic estimate is sixty-five percent of humanity.

Sixty-five percent.

The Watchers have no phrasing that makes the number less obscene.

The Other Sixty-Five Percent

A separate modern estimate places Vile Marks in approximately sixty-five percent of the population. Each Mark produces an ability shaped by the bearer as an individual. The numerical match to the Life's Blood death toll has resisted comfortable explanation.

No one currently knows why the figures match. The Watchers specifically warn against merging this mystery with a different unresolved phenomenon: the connection by which the Sins recognize one another. Sloth has an uncertain theory about that connection. He has never claimed it explains the Vile Mark percentage.

Sloth // response to direct Watcher question

No.

I don't know why the numbers match.

I have an idea about us.

That's not the same question.

Please don't make it the same question because it sounds better in a report.

What Survived

Sloth's archive is easy to reduce to injuries because the injuries are so extreme. That would repeat Pride's error: treating what happened to his body as more important than the person inside it.

He was the first to wake. The first to be lonely. The first to make a friend. The first to hear what Pride intended and decide that exhaustion was not a good enough reason to let someone else suffer in his place.

His most repeated word in the correction files is not a scream.

It is no.

Sloth // final line of deposition 21C

People say I was strong.

Didn't feel strong.

Felt tired.

Still said no.

Guess sometimes that's enough.
