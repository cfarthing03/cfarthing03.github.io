The Seven Virtues

Charity

Giving, sacrifice, generosity, and the belief that what you hold has meaning when it can help another.

Charity appears as a radiant winged goddess surrounded by gifts, keys, reliquaries, and hanging offerings: generosity rendered as ceremony.

The Open Hand

Charity is the virtue of giving without demanding equal return. Food, money, shelter, knowledge, time, and protection can all become offerings. A gift is sacred because the giver accepts having less so another person may have more.

Gifts as Divine Language

Her imagery is crowded with presents, keys, reliquaries, suspended treasures, and open hands. Her worship often treats objects symbolically: not as proof of wealth, but as proof that possession can be relinquished.

Sacrifice

Charity's followers often distinguish generosity from convenience. Giving what is easy is kind. Giving something that genuinely costs you is sacred. That doctrine makes her church capable of extraordinary mercy—and extraordinary pressure.

When Giving Becomes Ownership

The darkest version of Charity appears when a gift becomes a claim. Sacrifice can become martyrdom demanded by others. Help can become control. A person who is constantly told to give may eventually discover there is nothing left of them that was not promised away.
