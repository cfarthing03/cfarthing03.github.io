The Fourth Voice

Envy awakened into a laboratory that already contained three lessons.

Sloth had shown what open refusal cost. Lust had shown what fear could make someone build. Wrath had shown that power only made Pride more interested in finding a leash.

Envy chose not to show Pride what he could do.

His first independent form was an owl. With greater power he eventually adopted the humanoid shape associated with him later, but Pride's early assessments remained dismissive: observational aptitude, limited direct application, comparatively weak utility. The surviving Sins agree that this assessment was exactly what Envy wanted.

Pride // E-04 preliminary utility assessmentAwareness unusually high. Direct output disappointing. Subject watches procedures with persistent attention but demonstrates no function sufficient to justify priority access.

Continue low-level evaluation.

Watching the Watcher

Envy hid the true extent of his abilities and used them to observe Pride's work. Experiments. Schedules. Containment methods. Punishments. Supply routes. Which doors stayed open. Which records Pride trusted. Which failures made him angry enough to stop checking his assumptions.

Knowledge accumulated until observation became a map.

Envy

Pride's mistake was deciding usefulness meant being obvious.

If he thought I was weak, I could stand beside the answer while he looked somewhere else.

Watcher analysts have identified dozens of anomalies that now appear deliberate: measurements copied one digit wrong, components placed slightly out of tolerance, experimental conditions changed just enough to ruin reproducibility, warning signs redirected toward harmless failures while meaningful flaws remained hidden.

No single sabotage was large enough to justify scrutiny. Together, they ensured an increasing number of Pride's projects failed for reasons he could not isolate.

The Pattern

Sloth had once believed he would be alone forever. By Envy's awakening, none of them believed that anymore.

One relic had awakened. Then another. Then another. Envy treated repetition as evidence. If four could wake, the remaining relics probably would too.

That conclusion changed the problem. Escape no longer meant finding a way out for the people already conscious. It meant surviving long enough to avoid leaving the sleepers behind.

Envy began planning for people who did not yet exist.

ObservationPride's routines, security habits, experiment schedules, and control methods were catalogued rather than challenged openly.
KnowledgeInformation was retained across years until individual details became a complete model of how the laboratory functioned.
PlanningEscape preparations were designed around future awakenings and the need to move immediately once the final relic became conscious.
AspirationEnvy's later domain reflects the same instinct: observe what exists, understand how it works, then use that knowledge to make another possibility real.

The Plan No One Could See

No complete copy of Envy's original escape plan survives. That may be intentional. The fragments recovered by the Watchers are distributed across unrelated records, damaged inventories, copied schedules, and annotations that appear meaningless until compared side by side.

What can be established is simple: Envy intended to keep Pride's work unstable, protect the others from consequences where he could do so without exposing himself, and wait for the remaining relics to awaken.

He did not need to defeat Pride in the laboratory.

He needed Pride to believe he still controlled it until the moment control mattered.
