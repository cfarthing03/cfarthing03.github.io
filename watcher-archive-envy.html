The Fourth Voice

Envy awakened as an owl in his representative coloration and understood the situation faster than Pride expected.

By the fourth awakening, the pattern was difficult for the Sins to ignore. The conscious relics recognized one another immediately. Three had already awakened before Envy. Two relics remained silent. Envy's later testimony suggests he assumed those two would wake eventually long before there was proof.

Pride, meanwhile, concluded Envy was disappointing.

Pride // E-04 utility assessmentDirect output inferior to W-03. No obvious control value comparable to L-02. Observation capability present but limited. Subject is evasive when pressed for demonstration.

Priority reduced.

The assessment is wrong.

The Watchers believe Envy made it wrong on purpose.

Envy

Pride's first mistake with me was asking what I could do.

His second was believing the answer.

Convincing Pride There Was Nothing to See

Envy hid the true extent of his abilities. The Watchers still cannot state exactly where that concealment ended and deliberate misinformation began, which is almost certainly how Envy prefers it.

What can be proven is that Pride's records understate him while simultaneously containing errors that benefit the captives.

  • A reagent label is transposed on a test that would otherwise have produced a stable control compound. Pride blames an assistant. The handwriting is later matched to Envy.
  • A calibration value is copied one digit low, causing an experiment on Sloth-derived material to fail harmlessly instead of producing a viable sample.
  • A chamber scheduled for Lust's use is marked “mana contaminated” despite no measurable contamination. Pride postpones the session for twelve days.
  • Wrath's correction log lists a restraint failure. Mechanical inspection centuries later found the fastening pin had been filed from the inside.
  • Three inventory sheets show missing keys that reappear after Pride orders every room searched.

You missed some.

No, I'm not telling you which.

If I wanted a complete list, I would have kept one.

The Day Pride Almost Noticed

One sabotage came too close to being obvious.

A set of readings from a Sloth-derived experiment had been altered just enough to send Pride toward a useless conclusion. Pride repeated the experiment himself and obtained a different result. For the first time, his notes stop blaming assistants and begin asking whether a conscious relic might be interfering with records.

He questioned Envy for nearly nine hours.

SUS-E04-03 // Pride interrogation summaryE-04 denied ability to alter written information at distance. Denial remained consistent under threat. Subject displayed elevated pulse when S-01 was mentioned, not when punishment to self was described.

Possible attachment weakness. No proof of record interference.

Envy later admitted he could have ended the interrogation earlier by demonstrating more of what he could actually do.

He refused because proving usefulness would have solved one problem and created a permanent one.

Envy

He threatened me.

I was frightened.

I would like that sentence preserved because people keep making cleverness sound like absence of fear.

I was very afraid.

I lied anyway.

Pride eventually concluded Envy was hiding information, but not power. Envy gave him enough attitude, resentment, and partial truth to make that explanation feel complete.

Watcher annotationEnvy's best deceptions rarely required inventing a false person. He allowed Pride to see real irritation, real fear, and real envy—then let Pride assume those emotions explained everything else.

Watching the Watcher

Envy's resistance did not begin with an escape plan. It began with attention.

He learned Pride's habits: which keys he carried, which doors he checked himself, which measurements he trusted without repeating, which failures made him angry enough to become careless, which successes made him arrogant enough to stop looking for sabotage.

He learned the others too.

Sloth would endure almost anything but could be baited into resistance if someone else was threatened. Lust talked more when frightened and went silent when truly terrified. Wrath became easier to manipulate when he believed someone had been treated unfairly. Gluttony, once awake, would volunteer for pain if he thought it spared another person.

Envy did not catalogue these facts to exploit them.

He catalogued them because Pride already was.

Envy

There is a difference between knowing someone's weakness and deciding they are weak.

Pride never learned that.

A Plan Written Nowhere

No full copy of Envy's escape plan exists.

The Watchers believe that is deliberate.

Instead, pieces of the plan are distributed across documents that look unrelated: a maintenance schedule shifted by one day, a storage list with a false shortage, an instruction to move a restraint frame, a note that a west corridor lamp had begun failing, a corrected map whose “error” creates an unmonitored route between chambers.

Sabotage as Care

Envy rarely says he protected the others. The paperwork says it for him.

When Sloth's reconstruction stability dropped, a shipment needed for the next correction was suddenly “contaminated.” When Lust was approaching a major Rixel milestone and Pride planned a live resistance trial, the chamber's control lattice failed calibration three times. When Wrath's temporal output became dangerously unstable, Pride found a page of calculations that appeared to prove the entire experiment needed redesigning.

Some interventions bought hours. Some bought weeks. None freed anyone.

That limitation appears to have haunted Envy.

Envy

I could ruin a day.

Sometimes a month.

People like to call that heroic because they know the ending.

At the time it mostly felt like moving the knife to tomorrow.

The point of the sabotage was not victory. It was survival long enough for victory to become possible.

The Connection No One Explained

All relic-born Sins describe recognizing previously awakened Sins as their own kind. None can describe a sensory mechanism. Envy's testimony is the first to clearly use the phenomenon strategically.

He knew five were awake. He believed one remained.

Sloth is the only Sin who has ever suggested he may understand why this connection exists, and even he refuses to present his theory as certainty. Envy appears content with that.

Envy

I don't need to know why a bridge is there to know whether it holds.

Curiosity is useful.

Survival was urgent.

The Person Behind the Plan

Envy's file can make him look impossibly composed. The interview margins argue otherwise. He is sarcastic when embarrassed, evasive when praised, and becomes irritated whenever researchers describe his actions as selfless.

Envy // marginal exchange

Watcher: You risked yourself repeatedly for them.

Envy: I also needed them to escape.

Watcher: Those can both be true.

Envy: Yes. Annoying, isn't it?

His later domains—knowledge, observation, planning, aspiration—are visible here in an ugly early form. He survived because he noticed things Pride dismissed. He escaped because he wanted a future he had never seen.

Wanting what someone else has can become resentment.

For Envy, it became a plan.
