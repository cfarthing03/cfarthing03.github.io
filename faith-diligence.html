Religion of Diligence

The Faith of Diligence

The organized worship of Diligence, built around work, discipline, persistence, effort.

CULT PRACTICE // OBSERVED DILIGENCE

Diligence promises that effort can overcome weakness. Its cults take that beautiful idea and make failure feel like evidence that a person simply did not suffer enough for success.

THE PUBLIC FACE

Discipline, craftsmanship, training, resilience, and pride in work earned the hard way.

INITIATION

Members are given increasingly difficult tasks and expected to return after failure without complaint. Exhaustion is often celebrated as proof that the work mattered.

OBEDIENCE

Rest must be justified. Injury becomes a test. Asking someone else to carry part of the burden can be treated as surrendering the chance to prove devotion.

WHAT IT COSTS

People can work themselves sick while believing collapse is merely another obstacle to push through. The cult turns perseverance into a reason never to stop.

Core Teaching

The Faith of Diligence teaches that the answer to weakness is work. Skill, endurance, character, and even spiritual worth are believed to be things a person can build through sufficient effort. That belief is inspiring until every failure is interpreted as proof that the person has not worked hard enough yet.

Ordinary Worship

Training schedules, work quotas, repair duties, repeated drills, and the deliberate completion of unpleasant tasks are common devotion. Members are encouraged to document failures and return to them until the result improves, even when the return becomes physically punishing.

Temples and Clergy

Temples function like workshops, barracks, and schools. Clergy are expected to be visibly productive and may wear scars, calluses, or damaged tools as marks of service. A leader who rests too openly risks looking less devout than the people working beneath them.

Miracles and Divine Favor

Favor is associated with impossible stamina, sudden breakthroughs, and the ability to continue after exhaustion. This makes overwork easy to romanticize: a person nearing collapse may be congratulated for standing at the exact threshold where Diligence is expected to notice them.

The Dangerous Edge

The cult can make stopping feel immoral. Injury becomes weakness to overcome, rest becomes laziness, and asking for help becomes a lost opportunity to prove resilience. A virtue about persistence can quietly turn people into tools that feel guilty whenever they are not being used.
