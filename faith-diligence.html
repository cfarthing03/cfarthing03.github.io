Religion of Diligence

The Faith of Diligence

The organized worship of Diligence, built around work, discipline, persistence, effort.

LIVING FAITH

What worship looks like beyond doctrine

PRACTICE

Work sessions opened with intention, careful maintenance, training, and the ritual return to abandoned or failed work.

OFFERINGS & RITES

Finished work, repaired tools, records of failed attempts, and labor donated to someone who cannot repay it.

COMMUNITY

Guilds, workshops, schools, farms, kitchens, and training halls can all serve as religious spaces.

THE DEBATE

The great argument: is rest a necessary part of disciplined effort, or evidence that effort has stopped?

Core Teaching

Followers sanctify repetition, craft, training, maintenance, and the long labor required to build something that survives them.

Ordinary Worship

The faith does not require every follower to become clergy. Common devotion is expressed through craft, training, repair, repeated labor. The act matters most when it reflects deliberate commitment rather than public performance.

Temples and Clergy

Temples of Diligence use the same visual language seen in the god's iconography: chains, claws, moons, worn metal, and scars. Clergy are expected to embody the virtue publicly, which makes failures of doctrine especially politically and spiritually significant.

Miracles and Divine Favor

Miracles attributed to Diligence tend to be interpreted through the virtue itself. A believer does not merely ask for power; they ask for the ability to become more completely aligned with Diligence's ideal.

The Dangerous Edge

Its harshest sects can treat rest, disability, hesitation, or failure as moral weakness rather than part of being alive.

Work as Liturgy

Workshops, training halls, farms, kitchens, and construction sites can function as places of worship without becoming temples. Repetition performed with care is treated as liturgy. Many believers begin difficult work with a brief invocation and end by recording what failed so the next attempt begins with more knowledge.

Rites of Completion

Finishing a long task may be marked by deliberately leaving one small imperfection visible: a nick in a tool, an unpolished seam, a scar left uncovered. The custom reminds followers that completed does not mean effortless and that the history of the work should not be erased.

The Argument Over Rest

Diligence's clergy repeatedly fight over rest. Severe sects praise exhaustion as evidence of devotion. Reformist traditions call that theology wasteful and point out that a worker driven into collapse cannot continue the work. Entire monasteries and guilds have split over whether stopping can itself be disciplined.
