The Continent of Sin

Rushinghan

City of Lust

The jewel of the continent: temples, art, relationships, rebellion, and a culture built around caring without apology.

At the Table: Arriving in Rushinghan

Your players enter a city that feels emotionally loud. Music spills through open windows. Couples argue on balconies and reconcile ten minutes later in the street. Friends crowd around tables outside restaurants. Artists paint walls in public. Priests move between temples. Someone is laughing too loudly. Someone else is crying without trying to hide it.

Color is everywhere. Fabric, banners, flowers, painted signs, stained glass, murals, jewelry, street art, and religious decoration make neighborhoods feel intensely personal. Rushinghan looks lived in because residents are constantly leaving pieces of themselves on it.

The city feels warm even when the weather is not. People touch shoulders when they speak. Strangers compliment each other. Arguments become performances. Affection is public, grief is public, celebration is public. Rushinghan does not ask people to care less.

The Jewel of the Continent

Rushinghan is one of the continent's great cultural centers. Its reputation comes from art, music, nightlife, religion, relationships, public celebration, and the sheer intensity of life in its streets.

It is often called the jewel of the continent because of its beauty, but beauty alone misses the point. Rushinghan matters because people invest themselves in it. Neighborhoods have personalities. Temples have rivalries. Artists become local icons. Families remain tied to streets for generations.

A City of Temples

Rushinghan contains major temples dedicated to many of the Sins, making it one of the most important religious centers on the continent. Lust's temple remains central, but devotion to the rest of The Pantheon is visible throughout the city.

Religion regularly spills into public life. Festivals block streets. Weddings become neighborhood events. Memorials attract crowds. Processions move through markets. A person can pass from one temple district to another and feel the city change around them.

The Passions

Lust's followers, the Passions, shape the city's social philosophy. Their faith centers on love, desire, connection, consent, affection, and the freedom to choose who and what matters to you.

Because of Lust's history with the Rixel Virus, choice is not treated as a minor theological detail. It is fundamental. Connection without consent is control. Desire without choice is violation. Love means nothing if the person experiencing it was never allowed to say no.

Art and Expression

Creative expression is everywhere in Rushinghan. Musicians perform in taverns and plazas. Painters work publicly. Tailors treat clothing as identity. Dancers turn festivals into competitions. Poets recite work to crowds that are not always polite.

The city encourages people to make their inner lives visible. This can be beautiful, embarrassing, chaotic, and occasionally exhausting. Rushinghan has never cared much about looking restrained.

The Rebellion

Rushinghan became the heart of resistance during the rebellion against Pride. Tess emerged as one of the city's most important figures, helping transform emotion into organization.

The city did not rebel because its people stopped believing in the gods. It rebelled because they believed something they loved was being destroyed. In Rushinghan, caring deeply enough to fight is not a contradiction. It is almost expected.

Living in Rushinghan

Rushinghan can be overwhelming for people who prefer distance. Neighbors involve themselves in one another's lives. Relationships are discussed openly. Public affection is common. Community can feel supportive one moment and invasive the next.

For many residents, that closeness is exactly why they stay. The city assumes that attachment is not weakness. If something matters to you, there is no shame in admitting it.
